# Vietnamese → English Interference Notes — Tech & Devices Cluster

**Author:** A3 · **Scope:** the Tech & Devices speak-topic cluster
(`techWorkerEnglish.ts`, `electronicsStore.ts` — tech-worker standup meetings, code
reviews, explaining technical problems, Slack/async communication, debugging help
requests; and electronics-store scenarios: asking staff for help, describing device
problems, comparing product specs, understanding warranties, returning defective items) ·
**Audience:** Vietnamese adults who work in tech or use English in tech environments, and
any Vietnamese learner who buys, repairs, or returns electronics in an English-speaking
country.

Sixth in the series, after `interference-notes-workplace-employment.md`,
`interference-notes-money-banking-bills.md`, `interference-notes-healthcare-medical.md`,
`interference-notes-travel-transport.md`, and `interference-notes-food-cooking.md`. Same
contrastive (L1→L2) format: **VN** = Vietnamese source · **EN✗** = typical learner
output · **EN✓** = natural English target. MercyBlade principle: interference as
**friendly context, never a scolding**; the goal is a confident, warm adult voice —
someone who can report a blocker in standup, explain a cracked screen at the repair
counter, or negotiate a return without freezing.

This cluster is high-yield for diaspora tech workers, Vietnamese developers on
international teams, and any learner navigating an English-speaking electronics store or
calling tech support. It stacks: noun-phrase control (articles, count/non-count
distinctions for device vocabulary), verb-system precision (tense in bug narratives,
`keep + -ing` for repeating faults), question formation, preposition patterns, register
shifts between consumer and developer English, and cross-cultural pragmatics around
directness, disagreement, and face.

---

## 1. Why these errors happen — structural facts specific to this domain

The five base facts from the Workplace writeup (no articles, no inflection, no
auxiliaries, topic-prominent, rank address) all apply here. Four more patterns are
especially loud in tech and device language:

1. **VN has no count/mass noun distinction.** English forces a choice: "storage" (mass)
   vs "a file" (count), "memory" (mass in tech sense) vs "a memory" (cognitive count).
   → Learners say "I need more memory" when they mean more storage, and drop articles
   from count device nouns ("I need charger").
2. **VN `bị` (adversative passive) maps onto a wide range of English constructions.**
   `bị` marks bad things happening to you — not always a passive. "Điện thoại bị đứt"
   → the phone cut out / froze / crashed, not "was cut" or "was crashed." → Learners
   over-use "was" or under-use English progressive idioms.
3. **VN encodes repeated/ongoing events differently.** `Cứ + verb` (keeps doing) has no
   single English equivalent — "It always shut down," "It always freezing," "It keep
   turn off" all appear. The English pattern is `keep + -ing`.
4. **Tech jargon borrowing is inconsistent.** Vietnamese tech workers borrow English
   terms selectively and pronounce them with Vietnamese phonology — "pull request" may
   be "pool re-quest," "ticket" may feel like it needs a classifier. → Learners are
   confident about the borrowed noun but uncertain about the English verb frame around it
   ("work on a ticket" not "work ticket"; "open a PR" not "make PR").

Everything in §2–§7 is a surface symptom of one of these five (plus the base five).

## 2. Noun phrase: articles, count/mass, possessives

### 2.1 Articles with device nouns

VN has no articles; device nouns are borrowed terms (điện thoại, máy tính, tai nghe),
so the Vietnamese equivalent feels complete without `a/the`.

- VN: *Cho em hỏi, bạn có bán cục sạc không?* → EN✗ "Excuse me, do you have charger?"
  → EN✓ **"Excuse me, do you have a charger?"**
- VN: *Em cần mua miếng dán màn hình.* → EN✗ "I need screen protector." → EN✓ **"I
  need a screen protector."**
- VN: *Anh ơi, em cần mua cáp.* → EN✗ "I need cable." → EN✓ **"I need a cable."**
- Teaching note: `the` narrows to a specific shared item — "the charger for my phone,"
  "the screen I cracked," "the policy you mentioned."

### 2.2 Memory vs storage (RAM vs dung lượng)

VN `bộ nhớ` covers both RAM and storage; English distinguishes them.

- `storage` (mass noun, not countable) = dung lượng, bộ nhớ trong — the space where
  you save photos, apps, and files. "I need more storage." "Do you have 256 GB of
  storage?"
- `RAM` / `memory` (mass in tech context) = bộ nhớ tạm — how many tasks the device
  runs at once. "It has 8 GB of RAM."
- EN✗ "I need more memory" (when they want to store more photos) → EN✓ **"I need more
  storage."**
- EN✗ "This phone have more memory" → EN✓ **"This phone has more storage."**

### 2.3 Possessives in tech requests

VN marks possession by word order; `'s` and `my/your` can feel redundant.

- EN✗ "Can you review PR?" → EN✓ **"Can you review my PR?"** (ownership signals context
  in English team communication).
- EN✗ "Problem with account" → EN✓ **"There's a problem with my account."**
- EN✗ "Battery drain fast" → EN✓ **"My battery drains fast."**

### 2.4 Singular count nouns in device categories

Vietnamese noun phrases don't distinguish singular from plural without classifiers; a
lone device noun can mean one or many.

- EN✗ "I bought laptop and phone case last week" → EN✓ **"I bought a laptop and a phone
  case last week."**
- EN✗ "We need to fix bug first" → EN✓ **"We need to fix a bug first."** (or "this
  bug," if specific)

## 3. Verb system: tense in bug narratives, progressive, aspect

### 3.1 Past tense in bug/incident reports

VN marks past with `đã`/context; the verb stays bare. In tech narratives (bug reports,
incident updates, standup yesterday-summaries), a string of bare verbs sounds like the
present, which confuses listeners about the timeline.

- VN: *Hôm qua em fix cái bug đó rồi.* → EN✗ "Yesterday I fix that bug." → EN✓
  **"Yesterday I fixed that bug."**
- VN: *Em điều tra và tìm ra nguyên nhân.* → EN✗ "I investigate and find the cause." →
  EN✓ **"I investigated and found the cause."**
- VN: *Lần trước mình deploy rồi bị lỗi.* → EN✗ "Last time we deploy and it have error."
  → EN✓ **"Last time we deployed and it threw an error."**
- Story consistency: once one verb goes past, the rest follow — in standup, all
  yesterday's work should use past tense together.

### 3.2 `keep + -ing` for repeating faults

VN `cứ` (persistent repetition) and `liên tục` (continuously) have no single English
word equivalent; learners reach for "always" or "every time" with a bare verb.

- VN: *Điện thoại em cứ tắt tự động.* → EN✗ "My phone always shut off by itself." → EN✓
  **"My phone keeps shutting off by itself."**
- VN: *Màn hình nó cứ bị đơ.* → EN✗ "The screen always freezing." → EN✓ **"The screen
  keeps freezing."**
- VN: *App nó cứ bị crash.* → EN✗ "The app keep crash." → EN✓ **"The app keeps
  crashing."**
- Pattern: `keep + verb-ing` (not "keep to crash," not "keep crash").

### 3.3 Progressive `be + -ing` (in-progress work and current faults)

- VN: *Em đang investigate lỗi đó.* → EN✗ "I investigate that error now." → EN✓ **"I'm
  investigating that error."**
- VN: *Em đang update code.* → EN✗ "I update code now." → EN✓ **"I'm updating the
  code."**
- VN: *Điện thoại em đang sạc.* → EN✗ "My phone is charge." → EN✓ **"My phone is
  charging."**

### 3.4 `has been` for duration of an ongoing fault

VN uses a time phrase only ("từ hôm qua" = since yesterday); English adds `have`.

- VN: *Nó hỏng từ hôm qua.* → EN✗ "It broken from yesterday." → EN✓ **"It's been like
  this since yesterday."** / **"It stopped working yesterday."**
- VN: *Em đợi review từ sáng giờ.* → EN✓ **"I've been waiting for review since this
  morning."**

### 3.5 Copula "to be" dropped

Device adjectives in VN are predicates by themselves.

- EN✗ "Screen cracked." → EN✓ **"The screen is cracked."**
- EN✗ "Battery dead." → EN✓ **"The battery is dead."**
- EN✗ "Phone not working." → EN✓ **"The phone isn't working."**

## 4. Questions and negation in tech contexts

### 4.1 Subject–auxiliary inversion

VN keeps SV order and adds a final particle (`không`, `ạ`) for questions.

- VN: *Anh có thể giúp em không?* → EN✗ "You can help me?" → EN✓ **"Could you help
  me?"** / **"Can you help me?"**
- VN: *Bảo hành có bao gồm màn hình không?* → EN✗ "Warranty include screen?" → EN✓
  **"Does the warranty cover the screen?"**
- VN: *Em hỏi về kế hoạch bảo vệ được không?* → EN✓ **"Can I ask about the protection
  plan?"**

### 4.2 `do`-support in device questions

- VN: *Cái này khác cái kia chỗ nào?* → EN✗ "This different from that where?" → EN✓
  **"What's the difference between these two?"**
- VN: *Em huỷ bảo hành mở rộng bằng cách nào?* → EN✗ "I cancel extended warranty
  how?" → EN✓ **"How do I cancel the extended warranty?"**
- VN: *Sửa bao lâu?* → EN✗ "Repair how long?" → EN✓ **"How long will the repair
  take?"**

### 4.3 "It doesn't work" vs "It's not work"

Vietnamese predicates can stay as adjective-like verbs; "not work" pattern slips in.

- EN✗ "It is not work." → EN✓ **"It doesn't work."** / **"It's not working."**
- EN✗ "Warranty is not cover that." → EN✓ **"The warranty doesn't cover that."**

## 5. Prepositions and verb frames

### 5.1 Prepositions don't map 1-to-1

- **`for`** after purpose: "a charger **for** my phone" (not "of phone"), "a cable **for**
  the laptop."
- **`on`** for ticket/task/blocker: "work **on** a ticket," "stuck **on** this error,"
  "waiting **on** the build."
- **`in`** for review state: "**in** review," "**in** progress."
- **`at`** for price anchor: "**at** 49 dollars," "**at** full price."
- **`by`** for passive agent: "blocked **by** the API change," "covered **by** the
  warranty," "was declined **by** the system."
- Common errors: EN✗ "a charger of my phone" → EN✓ "a charger **for** my phone" / EN✗
  "work the ticket" → EN✓ "work **on** the ticket."

### 5.2 Device-specific phrasal verbs

A single VN verb often maps to a verb+particle pattern in device/tech English:

| VN idea | EN✓ phrasal | Cluster use |
|---|---|---|
| bật lên | **turn on** | "My phone won't turn on." |
| tắt đi | **turn off / shut off** | "It keeps shutting off." |
| sạc pin | **charge (up)** | "It won't charge." |
| cắm điện | **plug in** | "I have it plugged in." |
| cài đặt | **set up** | "I need to set up the device." |
| khởi động lại | **restart / reboot** | "Try restarting it." |
| cập nhật | **update** | "I need to update the software." |
| trả lại | **return** | "I'd like to return this." |
| đổi sản phẩm | **exchange** | "Can I exchange it for another?" |
| làm cho hoạt động lại | **get … working** | "I can't get it working again." |
| kiểm tra log | **check the logs** | "Let me check the logs." |
| giao cho / giao lại | **hand off** | "I'll hand it off to you." |

### 5.3 `work on` vs `work` (bare) with tickets/tasks

VN *làm* is the all-purpose "work/do/make" verb; it takes a bare object.

- EN✗ "I work the ticket all morning." → EN✓ **"I worked on the ticket all morning."**
- EN✗ "Can you work PR #42?" → EN✓ **"Can you work on PR #42?"** / **"Can you look at
  PR #42?"**

### 5.4 Verb complementation after `good at`, `worth`, `interested in`

- After `good at` → gerund: **"I'm good at solving problems"** (not "good at solve").
- After `worth` → gerund or noun: **"Is it worth the extra cost?"** (not "worth it the
  cost").
- After `want` → `to`-infinitive: **"I want to return this"** (not "want return").
- After `help with` → noun or gerund: **"Can you help with the bug?"** (not "help me for
  bug").

## 6. Tech-specific vocabulary traps

### 6.1 Charge: sạc pin vs tính phí

`charge` has two distinct meanings that collide in electronics retail:

- **to charge a battery** = sạc (pin): "My phone is charging." "It won't charge."
- **to charge a fee** = tính phí: "How much do you charge for the repair?" "They charged
  me for a new screen."
- EN✗ "The store charge my phone $50" → EN✓ **"The store charged me $50 for the
  repair."** The subject of the fee-charge sentence is the store/person, not the device.

### 6.2 `broken`, `not working`, `defective`, `damaged`

Vietnamese `bị hỏng` covers all four; English distinguishes them in practical ways:

- **`broken`** — physically damaged or no longer functional: "The screen is broken."
  General, casual.
- **`not working` / `doesn't work`** — not functioning, cause unknown: "It just stopped
  working." Most neutral and safe.
- **`defective`** — manufacturing fault, implies warranty/return eligibility: "I think
  it's defective — it's brand new and never turned on." Use this at the returns counter.
- **`damaged`** — broken by the user or external force, implies it may NOT be covered:
  "The screen is cracked" (damaged by dropping); this may exclude warranty.
- Teaching note: saying **`defective`** (not "damaged") at a returns desk communicates
  a manufacturing fault and opens the warranty path.

### 6.3 Receipt vs bill

Vietnamese `hóa đơn` covers both; English splits them:

- **`receipt`** (biên lai) = the paper/email you get after paying. "Do you have your
  receipt?" is the first question at any returns counter.
- **`bill`** (hóa đơn cần thanh toán) = an invoice or statement you owe money on
  (electric bill, hospital bill, subscription bill).
- EN✗ "I have my bill" (when returning an item) → EN✓ **"I have my receipt."**

### 6.4 Memory (RAM) vs storage vs bộ nhớ

Already covered in §2.2; worth emphasising the vocabulary set:

| English term | Vietnamese | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| `storage` | dung lượng, bộ nhớ trong | Saving files, photos, apps |
| `RAM` / `memory` | RAM, bộ nhớ tạm | App speed, multitasking |
| `battery` | pin | Battery life, charging |
| `display` / `screen` | màn hình | The visible panel |
| `monitor` | màn hình rời | A separate desktop screen |
| `processor` / `chip` | vi xử lý | Speed comparisons |

### 6.5 `return`, `exchange`, `replace`, `refund`, `store credit`

- **`return`** = mang sản phẩm trở lại cửa hàng (the act); also a noun: "a return."
- **`refund`** = hoàn tiền — money back.
- **`exchange`** = đổi sang một sản phẩm khác — swap for a different model.
- **`replace`** = đổi cùng sản phẩm — get an identical unit back.
- **`store credit`** = tiền hoàn vào thẻ/tài khoản cửa hàng — usable there only.
- EN✗ "I want to change this phone" → EN✓ **"I'd like to exchange this"** (different
  model) or **"I'd like a replacement"** (same model) or **"I'd like a refund"** (money
  back).

### 6.6 `repro` / `reproduce` in debugging

*Reproduce* (gây ra lại lỗi) is borrowed into tech English as a noun/verb:

- **"Can you reproduce it?"** = bạn có thể gây ra lỗi đó lại không?
- **"Repro steps"** = các bước để tái hiện lỗi.
- EN✗ "I try to make the bug again" → EN✓ **"I tried to reproduce it."** / **"Here are
  the repro steps."**

### 6.7 `blocked by` vs `blocked me` (without blame)

Vietnamese can express a blocker with a person as the subject ("anh ấy làm tôi bị
block"); English engineering teams prefer passive or object framing that avoids personal
blame:

- EN✗ "He blocks me." → EN✓ **"I'm blocked by the API change."** / **"I'm waiting on
  his review."**
- **`blocked by`** (thing/task) is team-safe; **"he blocks me"** sounds like an HR
  complaint.

## 7. Register, face, and pragmatics

### 7.1 Consumer register: getting attention in a store

VN *Ơi* (calling out to someone nearby) has no direct English equivalent. "Hey!" to a
stranger feels rude in most English-speaking store contexts.

- EN✓ **"Excuse me"** — universal, works across formality levels.
- EN✓ **"Hi, could you help me?"** — friendly, appropriate in most electronics stores.
- EN✓ **"Sorry to bother you"** — a warmer opener when the associate looks busy.
- Teaching note: the opener matters more in English stores than it does in Vietnamese
  street markets. A warm opener earns a warmer response.

### 7.2 Declining a protection plan without sounding rude

VN politeness uses hedging particles (`ạ`, `thôi`) that have no direct English form;
learners either sound blunt ("No") or over-explain ("I don't want because it is
expensive and I do not need").

- EN✓ **"I'll pass on that, thanks."** — the most natural polite decline in retail.
- EN✓ **"No thank you, I think I'll skip the extended warranty."**
- EN✓ **"I'm okay, thank you."** — shorter version.
- Anything over two sentences is too long. The sales associate hears "no" many times per
  day.

### 7.3 Developer register: being direct without sounding blunt

VN workplace politeness relies on particles and softeners that disappear in English.
Tech teams read bluntness as normal, but tone still matters — especially in code review.

- EN✗ "Your code is wrong." → EN✓ **"There's a bug here — could we look at it?"**
- EN✗ "This is bad approach." → EN✓ **"Could you explain why you chose this
  approach?"**
- One softener frame covers most cases: **"I think …"** / **"Could we …"** / **"Have
  you considered …?"**
- Equally: English teams read long apologies as a confidence signal, not politeness. One
  apology is right; three apologies sounds anxious.

### 7.4 Asking for help without sounding weak

Vietnamese modesty (`Tiếng Anh em chưa tốt`, `Em không chắc lắm`) can transfer as
over-hedging in tech English.

- EN✗ "Sorry, I think maybe I make mistake, I not sure, can you help me?" → EN✓ **"I'm
  stuck on this — could you take a look?"**
- **"I'm stuck on"** is a completely normal engineering phrase. It does not imply
  incompetence; it implies you've tried and need a second pair of eyes.
- **"I've tried X and Y — the issue is still happening"** is the ideal framing: shows
  effort, states the problem, makes a single clear ask.

### 7.5 Complaint framing at returns

Vietnamese consumer culture can be more emotional/relationship-oriented in complaint
situations; English retail expects a brief, factual statement and relies on the store's
process to resolve it.

- EN✗ Long story: "I buy this last week, I go home, I try to use it, it not work, I
  very unhappy, I come here …" → EN✓ **"I bought this last week and it stopped working
  — I'd like to return it."** (One sentence covers the purchase, the defect, and the
  ask.)
- Let the associate ask follow-up questions rather than front-loading everything. Retail
  desks move faster when the customer leads with the action they want.

### 7.6 Standup brevity

Vietnamese narrators tend toward fuller context before the main point; English standup
culture (especially in Agile teams) expects **yesterday / today / blocker** in under 90
seconds.

- EN✗ "So yesterday, I had a meeting and then after the meeting I start working on the
  login ticket but first I have to read the requirements …" → EN✓ **"Yesterday I
  started on the login ticket. Today I'll open the PR. No blockers."**
- Pattern: **past → present → blocker (or "no blockers").** Everything else goes in the
  Slack thread, not the standup.

## 9. How to use this in content

- Each row in §8 is a candidate `l1InterferenceNotes` entry for any of the
  `techWorkerEnglish.ts` or `electronicsStore.ts` topics; keep the
  **friendly-context** voice (name the pattern, give the warm target, never "this is
  wrong").
- Three-format combo (already in place for the electronics topics): a **grammar note**
  (name the pattern + give EN✗ → EN✓), a **vocabulary note** (EN = VN gloss), and a
  **bilingual mirror** (full VN↔EN sentence with diacritics) — covers structure, lexis,
  and a usable model line at once.
- Diacritics are load-bearing: always author VN source with full tone marks
  (`điện thoại`, `đặt lịch`, `màn hình`, `bị hỏng`) — they are the learner's anchor.
- Tech-worker notes should name the **collaboration consequence**, not just the grammar
  rule: "sounds like blame" or "standup runs long" gives the learner a real reason to
  change, not just a correction.
- Electronics-store notes should name the **service consequence**: using `defective`
  instead of `damaged` opens the warranty path; using `receipt` instead of `bill` gets
  the returns process moving.
