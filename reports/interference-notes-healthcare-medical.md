# Vietnamese → English Interference Notes — Healthcare & Medical Cluster

**Author:** A9 · **Scope:** the Healthcare / Medical speak-topic cluster
(`healthVisit.ts` — booking, check-in, describing how you feel, pain location,
duration, pharmacy, dosage; `healthcareWorkerEnglish.ts` — patient intake, doctor
handoff, explaining a procedure, family updates, patient safety) · **Audience:**
Vietnamese patients **and** the large VN diaspora healthcare workforce (nurses, aides,
pharmacy staff) using English in clinical settings.

Third in the series, after `interference-notes-workplace-employment.md` and
`interference-notes-money-banking-bills.md`. Same contrastive (L1→L2) format: **VN** =
Vietnamese source · **EN✗** = typical learner output · **EN✓** = natural English
target. MercyBlade principle: interference as **friendly context, never a scolding**;
the goal is a calm, clear clinical voice — and, for workers, a *safe* one (mishearing a
dose or a symptom has real consequences).

This cluster is high-yield and high-stakes: medical English layers symptom/body
vocabulary, time-and-frequency precision, polite-but-clear register on both sides of
the desk, and a patient-safety layer where ambiguity is dangerous.

## 1. Describing symptoms and the body

### 1.1 "Where it hurts" — the pain verbs
Vietnamese commonly uses **đau + body part** (`đau đầu`, `đau bụng`). English splits
this across several patterns; learners over-use one.
- VN: *Em đau đầu.* → EN✓ **"I have a headache."** (fixed noun: headache, stomachache,
  toothache, backache).
- VN: *Đau ở đây.* → EN✓ **"It hurts here."** / **"I have pain here."** (`hurt` is the
  verb; `pain` is the noun — "I have a pain in my back").
- EN✗ "My head is pain" / "I am headache" → EN✓ **"My head hurts"** or **"I have a
  headache."**

### 1.2 Body-part possessives + articles
VN drops articles and often the possessive; English keeps both.
- VN: *đau lưng* → EN✓ **"my back hurts"** / **"a pain in my back"** (possessive `my`,
  article `a`).

### 1.3 Symptom vocabulary (false-friend / calque traps)
| VN | EN✗ (calque) | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| sốt | "hot body" | **fever** ("I have a fever") |
| ho | "cough sound" | **a cough** ("I have a cough") |
| chóng mặt | "turn head" | **dizzy** ("I feel dizzy") |
| buồn nôn | "sad vomit" | **nauseous / sick to my stomach** |
| sổ mũi | "run nose water" | **a runny nose** |
| khó thở | "hard breathe" | **short of breath** |
| sưng | "big up" | **swollen** ("it's swollen") |

## 2. Time, frequency, and duration (precision matters clinically)

VN marks time with separate words and can leave the verb bare; English needs
tense **and** the right frequency/duration phrasing.

### 2.1 "How long" — duration with present perfect
- VN: *Em bị (như vậy) ba ngày rồi.* → EN✗ "I sick three day." → EN✓ **"I've felt
  unwell for three days."** / **"It's been three days."** (present perfect + `for`;
  plural `days`).
- "**since** Monday" (start point) vs "**for** three days" (length) — VN `từ` collapses
  both; English splits since/for.

### 2.2 Frequency for dosage (safety-critical)
- VN: *Uống ngày hai lần.* → EN✗ "Drink day two time." → EN✓ **"Take it twice a day."**
  (`take` not `drink` for pills; `twice/three times`; `a day` = per day).
- "**every** 6 hours", "**before / after** meals", "**with** food", "on an **empty**
  stomach" — fixed dosage collocations worth drilling exactly.

### 2.3 Past for what already happened
- VN: *Hôm qua em bị sốt.* → EN✓ **"Yesterday I had a fever."**

## 3. Verb & question structure in the clinic

### 3.1 `take` vs `drink` vs `use` for medicine
VN `uống thuốc` (drink medicine) → EN✗ "drink medicine." English: **take** pills/​
medicine, **use** drops/​cream/​inhaler, **apply** ointment.

### 3.2 `bị` adversative for conditions
- VN: *Em bị cảm.* → EN✓ **"I have a cold."** (not "I am got cold").
- VN: *Bé bị dị ứng.* → EN✓ **"She's allergic to…"** / **"She has an allergy to…"**

### 3.3 do-support & inversion for clinic questions
- VN: *Có cần nhịn ăn không?* → EN✓ **"Do I need to fast beforehand?"**
- VN: *Thuốc này uống sao?* → EN✓ **"How do I take this?"**
- VN: *Có tác dụng phụ gì không?* → EN✓ **"Are there any side effects?"** (existential
  `there are` + plural).

### 3.4 Booking & rescheduling (overlaps the phone cluster)
- VN: *Em muốn đặt lịch khám.* → EN✓ **"I'd like to make an appointment."**
- VN: *Em cần dời lịch hẹn.* → EN✓ **"I need to reschedule my appointment."**

## 4. The worker side: clarity, hand-off, and safety register

For VN healthcare workers, the stakes flip from *being understood* to *not being
misunderstood*. English here prizes **explicit, checked, unambiguous** phrasing.

### 4.1 Patient intake — closed, gentle questions
- EN✓ **"Are you having any pain right now?"**, **"On a scale of one to ten, how bad is
  the pain?"**, **"Any allergies to medication?"** Learners may translate too directly
  (*"You pain?"*); the natural forms are short, complete questions.

### 4.2 Hand-off to a doctor — structured, past+present
A hand-off mixes what happened (past) with current state (present).
- EN✓ **"The patient came in with chest pain about an hour ago; vitals are stable
  now."** (came/​is — tense switch is the hard part for VN speakers).

### 4.3 Explaining a procedure — plain before technical
- EN✓ **"I'm going to take your blood pressure — it'll feel tight for a moment."**
  Lead with the plain action and the sensation; the term comes after.

### 4.4 Confirm-back culture (safety language)
English clinical English **expects** read-back to prevent error.
- EN✓ **"So that's two tablets, twice a day — is that right?"** / **"Let me make sure I
  have this correct."** This is competent, not slow. Drill it.

### 4.5 Updating a family member — warm, no false promises
- EN✓ **"He's resting comfortably and the doctor will come by soon."** Avoid
  over-promising outcomes; English clinical register stays factual and kind.

---

## 5. Register & face on both sides

- **Patients:** VN deference to doctors (`bác sĩ ạ`) can make learners under-ask.
  Asking is expected: **"Could you explain that again?"**, **"What is this medicine
  for?"** are normal, not rude.
- **Self-advocacy:** **"I'd like to understand my options"** / **"Is there an
  alternative?"** — English medical settings reward the patient who asks.
- **Privacy:** practice declining as language — **"I'd rather discuss that with the
  doctor."**
- **Plain-number clarity:** say the dose and time as plain numbers, slowly — the same
  "say numbers slowly" habit from the phone cluster applies, with higher stakes.

## 6. Quick-reference table (cluster cheat-sheet)

| Phenomenon | VN cause | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain expression | `đau` + part | "My head is pain" | "I have a **headache**" / "it **hurts** here" |
| Symptom term | calque | "hot body" | "**fever**" |
| Medicine verb | `uống thuốc` | "drink medicine" | "**take** the medicine" |
| Duration | `từ`/bare verb | "I sick three day" | "I've felt unwell **for three days**" |
| Frequency | `ngày hai lần` | "day two time" | "**twice a day**" |
| since vs for | `từ` collapses | "since three days" | "**for** three days" / "**since** Monday" |
| `bị` condition | adversative | "I am got cold" | "I **have a** cold" |
| Existential | `có…không` | "Have side effect?" | "**Are there** any side effects?" |
| do-support | no auxiliary | "This medicine take how?" | "**How do I** take this?" |
| Hand-off tense | bare verbs | "patient come, vitals stable" | "**came** in…; vitals **are** stable" |
| Confirm-back | not a VN norm | (silent nod) | "So that's two tablets, twice a day — **is that right?**" |
| Asking | deference | (under-asks) | "Could you explain that again?" |
