# Vietnamese → English Interference Notes — Shopping & Services Cluster

**Author:** A4 · **Scope:** the Shopping & Services speak-topic clusters  
(`shopping.ts` — store help, price check, size and fit, out of stock, discounts, paying,
checkout, returns, exchanges, damaged items, online orders, grocery checkout;  
`services.ts` — laundry, haircut, tailoring, mobile phone plan, home internet, package
delivery, bank counter, ATM, motorbike repair, home repair, apartment management, house
cleaning, printing, local paperwork, gym membership;  
`haircutCustomer.ts`, `nailTechnicianEnglish.ts`, `autoCarRepairEnglish.ts`,
`gymFitnessCustomerEnglish.ts`, `postOffice.ts`, `phoneCustomerService.ts`)  
**Audience:** Vietnamese adults navigating retail, personal-care services, and
infrastructure services in English-speaking environments — diaspora families, new
arrivals, and IELTS/TOEIC candidates who need functional transactional English beyond the
classroom.

Seventh in the series, after `interference-notes-workplace-employment.md`,
`interference-notes-money-banking-bills.md`, `interference-notes-healthcare-medical.md`,
`interference-notes-travel-transport.md`, `interference-notes-food-cooking.md`, and
`interference-notes-tech-devices.md`. Same contrastive (L1→L2) format: **VN** =
Vietnamese source · **EN✗** = typical learner output · **EN✓** = natural English target.
MercyBlade principle: interference as **friendly context, never a scolding**; the goal is
a confident, warm adult voice — someone who can return a shirt without a receipt drama,
tip at a nail salon without freezing, or escalate a disputed phone bill without losing
composure.

## 1. Why these errors happen — structural facts specific to this cluster

The five base facts from the Workplace writeup (no articles, no inflection, no
auxiliaries, topic-prominent, rank address) all apply here. Six additional patterns are
especially prominent in shopping and service interactions:

1. **VN is a topic-prominent language; English expects a grammatical subject.** In a shop,
   learners say "This how much?" or "Return possible?" because the topic (`this`, `return`)
   leads the sentence and the predicate follows. English requires a grammatical subject +
   auxiliary + verb frame.
2. **VN lacks a grammatical tipping system.** Tipping is not embedded in Vietnamese service
   culture. The English vocabulary (`tip`, `gratuity`, `service charge`, `it's already
   included`) refers to a social contract that learners have to learn from scratch — the
   grammar is the easy part; the cultural frame is the hard part.
3. **VN service transactions are higher-contact and more relationship-oriented.** Learners
   may over-explain before the main request, use formality markers (`ạ`) that have no
   English equivalent, or interpret an English "No problem" as a dismissal rather than a
   reassurance.
4. **VN complaint culture depends on tone and face-saving;** direct complaint language in
   English feels riskier than it actually is. "This is not what I asked for" is not rude in
   English retail — it is a factual repair opener. Learners soften too much ("I think
   maybe…") or, if they overcorrect, sound blunt ("You did it wrong").
5. **VN service vocabulary has fewer lexical splits.** Single Vietnamese words cover
   distinction pairs that English separates: `giặt` (wash/launder/dry-clean → different
   services), `cắt` (cut/trim/layer → different results), `sửa` (fix/alter/repair →
   different trades), `đặt` (book/reserve/order → different industries). Learners pick the
   wrong English word and get the wrong service.
6. **Phone interactions are especially hard.** VN learners have fewer practice opportunities
   with automated phone menus, hold music, and live agent handoffs. The listening
   challenge (fast speech, phone audio quality) compounds the speaking challenge, and many
   avoid calls entirely. The skills (`phoneCustomerService.ts`) are high-yield precisely
   because they transfer to every other service domain.

## 2. Noun phrase in retail and service contexts

### 2.1 Articles on retail nouns

Vietnamese has no articles; product and service nouns appear bare in Vietnamese. In
English, most countable service/product nouns need `a/an` (first mention, non-specific)
or `the` (specific or shared reference).

- VN: *Cho tôi xem túi.* → EN✗ "Can I see bag?" → EN✓ **"Can I see the bag?"** (specific
  bag on the shelf) / **"Can I see a bag?"** (one from a selection)
- VN: *Tôi cần hẹn giờ.* → EN✗ "I need appointment." → EN✓ **"I need an appointment."**
- VN: *Mình muốn cắt ngắn phần sau.* → EN✗ "I want trim back." → EN✓ **"I'd like a trim
  in the back."**
- VN: *Có thể cho tôi biên lai không?* → EN✗ "Can I have receipt?" → EN✓ **"Can I have a
  receipt?"** (non-specific) or **"Can I have the receipt?"** (the one just printed)
- Teaching note: `the` for shared, specific items; `a/an` for any one of a class; no
  article only for mass nouns (`change`, `cash`, `laundry`) and proper service names
  (`dry cleaning`, `alterations`).

### 2.2 Count/mass distinctions in service vocabulary

Several key service nouns behave as mass nouns in English but feel like countable objects
in Vietnamese:

| Mass in English | VN mental model | Common error | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| `laundry` | each piece = a unit | "My laundries" | "My laundry" |
| `dry cleaning` | the service = a thing | "Do dry cleanings" | "Get / drop off dry cleaning" |
| `alterations` (always plural) | one change = one unit | "One alteration done today" | "Are the alterations ready?" |
| `change` (coins/bills) | each coin = one piece | "Give me changes" | "Can I have change?" |
| `postage` | each stamp feeling | "How much are postages?" | "How much is postage?" |
| `cash` | notes = plural pieces | "I have cashes" | "I have cash" |

### 2.3 Possessives signal relationship in service contexts

VN word-order possession (`cái túi của tôi` = my bag, word-order only) can drop the
English possessive marker.

- EN✗ "Can you fix hem?" → EN✓ **"Can you fix the hem?"** (specific hem of the garment
  just shown) or **"Can you fix my hem?"** (ownership explicit)
- EN✗ "What about order?" → EN✓ **"What about my order?"**
- EN✗ "I am here for car." → EN✓ **"I'm here for my car."**

## 3. Verb system in transactional English

### 3.1 `would like` vs `want` — the single most important register shift

`Tôi muốn` = I want. In casual contexts this is fine; in service transactions (retail,
salons, counters, phone agents) English speakers expect `I'd like` or `I would like`
because it is softer and implies the service provider has the ability to say no — the
normal service interaction frame.

- EN✗ "I want to return this shirt." → EN✓ **"I'd like to return this shirt."**
- EN✗ "I want haircut, short sides." → EN✓ **"I'd like a haircut — short on the sides."**
- EN✗ "I want cancel membership." → EN✓ **"I'd like to cancel my membership."**
- EN✗ "I want speak to manager." → EN✓ **"I'd like to speak to a manager."**
- Teaching note: `want` is not rude, but `I'd like` signals social fluency. It is one of
  the highest-ROI single phrases for any Vietnamese learner entering service contexts.

### 3.2 Modal ladder for requests

VN learners tend to use `can` (the first modal they learned) for all service requests.
English uses a ladder from casual to formal:

- `Can you…` — casual, familiar, for staff you know or in informal settings.
- `Could you…` — standard polite for most service contexts.
- `Would you be able to…` — formal, for difficult requests or escalations.
- `Is it possible to…` — gentle, useful when you're not sure if the service exists.

Mixing levels is fine; a single `Could you` lifts the register noticeably.

### 3.3 Tense in service narratives

When explaining a problem — a wrong order, a damaged item, a billing dispute — a string
of present-tense verbs makes the timeline ambiguous.

- VN: *Em đặt lịch lúc 3 giờ, giờ đã 3:30 rồi mà chưa được gọi.* → EN✗ "I book 3
  o'clock, now is 3:30, nobody call me." → EN✓ **"I had an appointment at 3 — it's 3:30
  now and I haven't been called yet."**
- VN: *Tôi mua cái này tuần trước, về nhà thấy nó bị hỏng.* → EN✗ "I buy last week, I
  go home, it broken." → EN✓ **"I bought this last week and it was broken when I got
  home."**
- VN: *Họ tính tiền tôi hai lần.* → EN✗ "They charge me two time." → EN✓ **"They charged
  me twice."**

### 3.4 `get` + past participle for service outcomes

English service language uses `get + past participle` as a natural passive for things you
arrange to happen to objects or yourself. VN learners may use plain present or drop the
passive entirely.

- EN✓ **"I need to get this altered."** (not "I need alter this") — alterations / tailoring
- EN✓ **"I want to get my car serviced."** (not "I want service my car")
- EN✓ **"I'd like to get a trim."** (not "I want trim hair")
- EN✓ **"Can I get this dry-cleaned?"** (not "Can you dry cleaning this?")
- Pattern: `get + object + past participle` (get it fixed, get it cleaned, get it done).

### 3.5 `have been` for duration of a problem

Time phrases alone (VN: "từ hôm qua," "2 tiếng rồi") do not form a complete English
sentence for ongoing problems.

- VN: *Tôi chờ 45 phút rồi.* → EN✗ "I wait 45 minutes already." → EN✓ **"I've been
  waiting for 45 minutes."**
- VN: *Dịch vụ internet của tôi bị lỗi từ sáng.* → EN✗ "Internet broken from morning." →
  EN✓ **"My internet has been down since this morning."**
- VN: *Đơn hàng của tôi trễ 3 ngày rồi.* → EN✗ "Order late 3 days." → EN✓ **"My order
  is 3 days late."** / **"My order has been delayed by 3 days."**

## 4. Questions and clarification in service contexts

### 4.1 Wh-question word order

Vietnamese question words (`bao nhiêu`, `ở đâu`, `bao giờ`) appear at the end or middle
of the sentence without the S-V inversion English requires.

- VN: *Cái này bao nhiêu?* → EN✗ "This how much?" → EN✓ **"How much is this?"**
- VN: *Đặt lịch ở đâu?* → EN✗ "Book appointment where?" → EN✓ **"Where can I book an
  appointment?"**
- VN: *Khi nào xong?* → EN✗ "When finish?" → EN✓ **"When will it be ready?"**
- VN: *Hem ngắn thêm bao nhiêu?* → EN✗ "Shorten hem how much?" → EN✓ **"How much would
  you shorten the hem?"**

### 4.2 "How long will it take?" — the service timeline question

A high-frequency question in every service context; VN learners often word it as a
statement or a fragment.

- EN✗ "The repair how long?" → EN✓ **"How long will the repair take?"**
- EN✗ "Dry cleaning finish when?" → EN✓ **"When will the dry cleaning be ready?"**
- EN✗ "Appointment how long?" → EN✓ **"How long is the appointment?"** / **"How long will
  it take?"**
- Teaching note: `take` (for time) is the key verb: "It takes 30 minutes," "How long will
  it take?" VN has no single-word equivalent — `mất` (lose/take) can feel like "waste,"
  which causes hesitation.

### 4.3 Asking for repetition and clarification

Learners often freeze when they don't understand and say nothing, or say "Sorry" and wait.
English customer-service registers expect explicit clarification requests.

- VN: (silent) → EN✓ **"Sorry, could you repeat that?"**
- VN: *Em không hiểu.* → EN✗ "I don't understand." (fine, but incomplete as a repair
  strategy) → EN✓ **"Sorry, I didn't catch that — could you say it more slowly?"**
- For phone calls: EN✓ **"Could you spell that for me?"** / **"Sorry, it's a bit noisy —
  could you say that again?"**
- The word `catch` is idiomatic: "I didn't catch that" = I didn't hear/understand it.

### 4.4 Negotiating on the phone: holds and transfers

- VN: *Cho tôi giữ máy à?* → EN✗ "You put me hold?" → EN✓ **"Am I on hold?"** / **"Are
  you putting me on hold?"**
- EN✓ **"How long will I be on hold?"**
- EN✓ **"Could you transfer me to someone who can help?"**
- EN✓ **"I've been on hold for 20 minutes — is there someone available?"**
- Teaching note: `on hold` is a fixed phrase (not "in hold" or "at hold").

## 5. Prepositions and verb frames in service contexts

### 5.1 Payment prepositions

- `pay **in** cash` (the currency medium) — VN: `trả bằng tiền mặt`
- `pay **by** card` / `pay **with** a card` (the instrument) — both correct
- EN✗ "pay by cash" → EN✓ **"pay in cash"** (the most common native form in retail)
- EN✗ "pay with cash money" → EN✓ **"pay in cash"** (no need for "money")

### 5.2 Service verb frames (preposition patterns)

| Frame | Use in service context | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `ask **for**` | requesting a thing | "Can I ask **for** a bag?" |
| `look **for**` | searching | "I'm looking **for** a medium." |
| `wait **for**` | queuing/timing | "I've been waiting **for** 20 minutes." |
| `exchange **for**` | swapping items | "I'd like to exchange this **for** a larger size." |
| `return **to**` | sending back | "I'd like to return this **to** the store." |
| `sign **up for**` | enrolling | "I'd like to sign **up for** the gym." |
| `cancel` (no preposition) | ending a service | "I'd like to cancel my membership." |
| `drop **off**` | leaving for service | "I need to drop **off** some dry cleaning." |
| `pick **up**` | collecting | "I'm here to pick **up** my laundry." |
| `tip` (no preposition) | gratuity | "Should I tip here?" (not "tip to") |

### 5.3 Phrasal verbs for service actions

| VN idea | EN✓ phrasal | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Đặt lịch | **book / make an appointment** | haircut, car service, gym |
| Hủy lịch | **cancel the appointment** | all services |
| Thay đổi lịch | **reschedule** | all services |
| Giao hàng | **drop off / deliver** | laundry, packages |
| Nhận hàng | **pick up / collect** | laundry, car, parcels |
| Chờ đến lượt | **wait in line / wait your turn** | any counter |
| Xếp hàng | **queue up / get in line** | post office, bank |
| Lấy số | **take a number** | service counters with ticket machines |
| Thêm vào | **add (a service) on** | haircut extras, gym add-ons |
| Bỏ qua (không muốn) | **skip / pass on** | declining an add-on |

## 6. Vocabulary traps by sub-cluster

### 6.1 Retail: receipt, bill, invoice, refund, store credit

Vietnamese `hóa đơn` and `biên lai` blur across four English terms:

- **`receipt`** (biên lai nhận hàng) = the paper/email you get after buying. Needed at
  the returns counter: "Do you have your receipt?"
- **`bill`** (hóa đơn phải trả) = a statement of money owed (phone bill, electric bill).
  Do not say "I have my bill" when returning an item.
- **`invoice`** (hóa đơn thương mại) = formal business document, typically B2B.
- **`refund`** (hoàn tiền) = money returned to you.
- **`store credit`** (tiền tín dụng cửa hàng) = credit usable only at that store —
  not a refund.
- EN✗ "I want my money exchange." → EN✓ **"I'd like a refund."** (money back) or
  **"I'd like to exchange it."** (different item)

### 6.2 Haircut: the vocabulary of a style request

`cắt tóc` covers the whole haircut encounter; English splits it into distinct terms:

| English term | VN equivalent | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| `haircut` | cắt tóc | The general service |
| `trim` | cắt tỉa / cắt nhẹ | Small amount off, maintaining length |
| `layers` | phân lớp | Cutting hair at different lengths for volume |
| `taper` | vuốt ngắn dần | Graduation, common in men's styles |
| `fade` | cạo nhạt dần | Close-cut graduation, US common |
| `blunt cut` | cắt thẳng ngang | All one length, no layers |
| `fringe / bangs` | tóc mái | Front fringe |
| `undercut` | phần dưới cạo ngắn | Shaved underneath |

- EN✗ "I want cut like this, not too short, a little off." → EN✓ **"I'd like a trim —
  just a little off the length, please."**
- EN✗ "I want the hair different, some long some short." → EN✓ **"I'd like layers."**
- Teaching note: showing a photo bridges vocabulary gaps fast. "Something like this" while
  showing a photo is perfectly normal at any salon.

### 6.3 Nail salon: the service lexicon (both client and technician sides)

`nailTechnicianEnglish.ts` covers the technician's English with clients; `vnService.ts`
includes a client-side scenario. Key terms:

| English term | VN equivalent | Context |
|---|---|---|
| `manicure` | làm móng tay | Nails + cuticles + polish |
| `pedicure` | làm móng chân | Foot nails |
| `gel polish` | sơn gel | Longer-lasting, needs UV cure |
| `acrylic` | đắp bột acrylic | Extensions/overlays |
| `dip powder` | bột nhúng | Alternative to gel/acrylic |
| `nail art` | vẽ móng | Designs on nails |
| `cuticle` | da hãm móng | The skin at the nail base |
| `filing` | dũa móng | Shaping the nail edge |
| `soak off` | ngâm tẩy | Removing gel/acrylic |
| `fill in / infill` | đắp thêm lớp mới | Maintenance on grown-out acrylics |

- EN✗ (client) "I want do nails, the special one." → EN✓ **"I'd like a full set of
  acrylics."** / **"I'd like gel nails."**
- EN✗ (tech) "You want what color?" → EN✓ **"Do you have a color in mind?"** /
  **"What color were you thinking?"**
- Technician frame: questions like "How short would you like them?" and "Are you okay with
  the shape?" show professional fluency without needing complex grammar.

### 6.4 Tipping — the cultural gap

Vietnamese service culture does not have an embedded tipping norm (some tourism contexts
aside). In North American English-speaking contexts, the absence of a tip reads as
dissatisfaction; in Australian/UK contexts, tipping is less expected. Learners need both
the vocabulary and the cultural map.

- **Tip** (noun/verb): "Is the tip included?" / "I'd like to add a tip." / "Can I tip in
  cash?" No preposition needed: tip the driver, tip the technician.
- **Gratuity** = formal word for tip, often printed on restaurant bills.
- **Service charge** = a mandatory fee added by the establishment; NOT the same as a
  voluntary tip.
- EN✗ "Is service included?" (ambiguous) → EN✓ **"Is the tip included?"** or **"Does
  the total include gratuity?"**
- EN✗ "I give tip to card machine?" → EN✓ **"Can I add a tip on the card?"**
- Teaching note: in North American nail/hair salons, tipping 15–20% in cash is the
  strong norm. Saying "Can I tip in cash separately?" is a completely normal question.

### 6.5 Car repair: describing symptoms vs causes

`autoCarRepairEnglish.ts` focuses on the customer-side visit to a repair shop. Key trap:
learners state a cause ("I think the engine problem") rather than a symptom ("It makes a
noise when I brake").

- EN✗ "My car engine problem." → EN✓ **"There's something wrong with the engine."** /
  **"The engine warning light came on."**
- EN✗ "The brake have noise." → EN✓ **"The brakes are making a grinding noise."**
- EN✗ "Car don't start." → EN✓ **"The car won't start."** (modal + base verb for refusal)
- EN✗ "I think oil change need." → EN✓ **"I think it needs an oil change."**
- Symptom pattern: **"It [verb phrase] when I [action]."** — "It vibrates when I brake."
  "It stalls when I stop." "It pulls to the left when I accelerate." This pattern covers
  most car complaint scenarios.
- Key vocabulary: `estimate` (báo giá trước), `invoice` (hóa đơn sau khi sửa), `warranty
  on parts and labor` (bảo hành cho phụ tùng và công lao động), `loaner car` (xe mượn
  tạm), `diagnostic fee` (phí chẩn đoán).

### 6.6 Gym: membership and cancellation language

`gymFitnessCustomerEnglish.ts` covers joining, using equipment, classes, trainers, and
cancellation. The trap is legal/contract language (monthly, annual, notice period).

- EN✗ "I want stop gym." → EN✓ **"I'd like to cancel my membership."**
- EN✗ "What month I pay?" → EN✓ **"How much is the monthly fee?"**
- EN✗ "Can I free trial?" → EN✓ **"Do you offer a free trial?"** / **"Is there a trial
  period?"**
- EN✗ "I want change my plan." → EN✓ **"I'd like to change my membership plan."** /
  **"I'd like to downgrade my plan."**
- Key vocabulary: `contract` (hợp đồng), `month-to-month` (tháng tháng, không ràng buộc),
  `notice period` (thời gian báo trước khi hủy — e.g., "30 days' notice"), `freeze`
  (tạm dừng membership, không hủy), `auto-renew` (tự động gia hạn).
- Teaching note: "freeze" as a gym term is important — learners who don't know it may
  cancel (and lose their rate) when pausing would have been enough.

### 6.7 Post office: shipping vocabulary

`postOffice.ts` covers domestic parcels, international packages, registered mail, and
missing packages. The traps are shipping-method names and customs vocabulary.

- EN✗ "I want send this to Viet Nam." → EN✓ **"I'd like to send this to Vietnam."**
- EN✗ "What is cheapest send?" → EN✓ **"What's the cheapest shipping option?"**
- EN✗ "How many day it arrive?" → EN✓ **"How many days will it take to arrive?"** /
  **"When is the estimated delivery?"**
- Shipping vocabulary: `first class` (ưu tiên) · `priority mail` (bưu kiện ưu tiên) ·
  `registered mail` (thư bảo đảm) · `certified mail` (thư có xác nhận nhận) ·
  `tracking number` (số theo dõi) · `customs declaration` (tờ khai hải quan) ·
  `declared value` (giá trị khai báo) · `fragile` (dễ vỡ).
- EN✗ "I write what inside?" → EN✓ **"What do I write for the contents?"** / **"What
  should I declare?"**

### 6.8 Phone customer service: scripted phrases that unblock calls

`phoneCustomerService.ts` covers calling a provider, disputing a bill, navigating menus,
and handling holds. The trap is that learners know what they want but not the register
phrases agents expect.

| Situation | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | "Hello I have problem." | **"Hi, I'm calling about a problem with my account."** |
| Account ID | (silence or confusion) | **"My account number is…"** / **"My name is… and the last four of my SSN is…"** |
| Bill dispute | "You charge me wrong." | **"There's a charge on my bill I don't recognize."** |
| Escalation | "Give me manager." | **"I'd like to speak with a manager, please."** |
| Hold | "How long I wait?" | **"How long will the wait be?"** |
| Transfer | "You send me another?" | **"Could you transfer me to the right department?"** |
| Confirm | "Okay okay, okay." | **"Just to confirm — you'll credit my account by…, is that right?"** |
| Closing | (hang up) | **"Thank you for your help — I'll note your name just in case."** |

## 7. Register, face, and pragmatics

### 7.1 Declining add-ons politely

In retail and service encounters, staff offer upgrades, add-ons, protection plans, and
extended services. Vietnamese learners either say nothing (and get charged) or say a flat
"No" (and sound abrupt).

- EN✓ **"I'll pass on that, thanks."** — universal, works in every retail context.
- EN✓ **"No thank you, just the standard service."**
- EN✓ **"I'm okay, thank you."**
- Teaching note: declining is socially normal. The staff member asks everyone. One warm
  sentence is all that's needed.

### 7.2 Complaining without confrontation

VN complaint interactions often mix apology, explanation, and hope the other person
figures it out. English service complaints are more direct and less personal — naming the
problem factually opens the resolution process faster.

- EN✗ "Sorry, I not sure but I think maybe this is wrong?" → EN✓ **"I think there's a
  mistake on my bill."**
- EN✗ "I wait a long time, it is difficult for me." → EN✓ **"I've been waiting longer
  than expected — could you check on my order?"**
- EN✗ "The hem not same like I say before." → EN✓ **"This isn't quite what I asked for —
  could you fix the hem a bit more?"**
- Teaching note: naming the issue + making ONE specific ask is the winning structure.
  Long narratives and multiple complaints slow the interaction; staff are trained to
  respond to clear single requests.

### 7.3 Saying you didn't understand something during a service

Mid-service misunderstanding is common. Stopping politely mid-way is better than
discovering later you got the wrong service.

- EN✓ **"Sorry — could you show me what you mean?"** (during a haircut or fitting)
- EN✓ **"Could you go over that again?"** (during a phone call)
- EN✓ **"I'm not sure I follow — could you explain that part?"**
- EN✓ **"Actually — I think I wanted something slightly different."** (when you realize
  mid-service)
- Teaching note: intervening mid-service feels rude in some Vietnamese contexts
  (interrupting while someone is working), but in English service culture it is expected
  and welcomed — it prevents a bigger problem later.

### 7.4 Cultural load of "No problem" and "Of course"

Staff say "No problem" or "Of course" as reassurance, not as a neutral fact-statement.
Learners sometimes misread "No problem" as "you were bothering me and I'm saying it's
fine" (over-apologetic response), or "Of course" as slightly condescending.

- After hearing "No problem": EN✓ simply **"Thank you."** — no need for another apology.
- After hearing "Of course": EN✓ simply **"Great, thank you."**
- Teaching note: these are service-culture warmth phrases. Responding with another "Sorry
  for the trouble" lengthens the interaction and signals the learner hasn't absorbed the
  reassurance.

### 7.5 Opening a service interaction warmly without over-hedging

Vietnamese learners often open with a long context-setting explanation before the main
ask, influenced by VN narrative structure and face concerns. English service staff expect
the request early, with context as a follow-up if asked.

- EN✗ "Hello, um, sorry to bother you, I have a question, I don't know if it's okay to
  ask, I came here before and I have some clothes and I think I want to maybe clean them."
  → EN✓ **"Hi — I'd like to drop off some clothes for dry cleaning. Do you have a price
  list?"**
- Pattern: **greeting + I'd like to + [service request] + [one detail if needed].**
  Everything else is a response to a follow-up question.

### 7.6 Responding to "Can I help you?" without freezing

The standard store opener catches learners off guard if they were still preparing.

- EN✓ **"Yes, thanks — I'm looking for [item]."**
- EN✓ **"Actually, I'm just browsing — thank you."** (if not ready to ask)
- EN✓ **"Hi yes — could you help me find…?"**
- Teaching note: "just browsing" is the polite way to say you don't need help yet. Many
  learners don't know this phrase and feel cornered into buying or explaining themselves.

## 9. How to use this in content

- Each row in §8 is a candidate `l1InterferenceNotes` entry for any of the covered topic
  files; keep the **friendly-context** voice — name the pattern, give the warm target,
  never frame it as a mistake list.
- **Three-format combo** already in use for the deepened topics: a **grammar note** (VN
  cause + EN✗ → EN✓), a **vocabulary note** (EN term = VN gloss), and a **bilingual
  mirror** (full sentence with VN diacritics). All three types are represented in §6–§7.
- **Diacritics are load-bearing.** Always author VN source with full tone marks
  (`đặt lịch`, `cắt tóc`, `hoàn tiền`, `biên lai`, `hỏng rồi`) — they are the learner's
  anchor to their own L1 form.
- **Service-consequence framing** lifts engagement: "Using 'I'd like to cancel' instead
  of 'I want stop gym' tells the agent you know what you're asking for — the process
  starts faster" gives the learner a reason beyond grammar correction.
- **Tipping notes** should be pragmatic, not prescriptive — "In North American nail/hair
  salons, learners who know 'Can I tip in cash?' feel more confident and less awkward"
  is more motivating than "You must tip 15–20%."
- **Phone service notes** should name the listening challenge as real: "Phone calls are
  hard because of audio quality AND speed; the goal is to have 3–4 key phrases ready before
  you dial, not to understand every word." Reduces avoidance anxiety.
