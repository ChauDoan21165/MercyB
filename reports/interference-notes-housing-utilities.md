Housing is the highest-stakes domain after employment. Errors here have real
consequences: an unclear repair request goes unread; a wrong deposit question loses
money; a lease misread creates legal problems. Vietnamese learners face two structural
challenges specific to this domain: (1) the rich VN classifier system (căn, tòa, phòng,
gian) has no English parallel — every housing noun drops to its bare form; (2) VN
politeness is carried by sentence-final particles (ạ, nhé, dạ) and kinship address,
while English politeness is carried by modal verbs (could, would, I'd like) and
explicit hedges. The shift is learnable and worth drilling thoroughly.

## 1. Why these errors happen — structural facts specific to this domain

Five base facts explain most housing and utilities errors:

1. **VN uses classifiers; English does not.** Vietnamese counts and refers to objects
   with a classifier word before the noun: `căn nhà` (unit-house = apartment/house),
   `tòa nhà` (tower-building = building), `phòng trọ` (room-lodging = room for rent),
   `căn hộ` (unit-dwelling = apartment/condo). English drops these entirely — the noun
   stands alone or with an article. → learners say "one room" when they mean "one
   apartment," or omit the noun type entirely because the classifier felt like enough.

2. **VN copula is optional.** `Nhà tôi bị rò nước` (House mine PASSIVE leak water)
   has no equivalent of "is." English requires a verb: "My apartment is leaking." →
   learners drop "is/are/was" in repair descriptions and building questions.

3. **VN has no progressive aspect marker.** "Đang rò" just means the action is ongoing;
   there is no -ing form. English uses `is + -ing` to signal something happening right
   now: "is leaking," "is not working." → learners say "The sink leak" or "Heater not
   work" for currently active problems.

4. **VN politeness is particle-based.** `Ạ`, `nhé`, `dạ`, `vâng` are sentence-final
   softeners and acknowledgments. English politeness is modal-based: `Could you…?`,
   `Would it be possible to…?`, `I'd like to…`, `Would you mind…?`. Learners who know
   these forms often drop them under pressure; learners who don't know them may come
   across as blunt even when trying to be polite.

5. **VN bundles utilities; English separates them.** `Điện nước` (electricity-water)
   is a single phrase covering all household utilities. In English, electricity, gas,
   and water are three separate accounts, often with three different companies. → "I
   want to set up điện nước" becomes "I want to set up electricity" (one of several),
   not "I want to set up my utilities" (all together).

## 2. Classifiers → English housing nouns

### 2.1 The classifier map

Vietnamese housing language uses classifiers that carry meaning English puts directly in
the noun. Knowing the equivalent English noun is essential for every conversation.

| VN classifier phrase | Meaning | EN✓ noun |
|---|---|---|
| Căn nhà / Căn hộ | standalone residential unit | **apartment** / **unit** / **condo** (owned) |
| Phòng trọ | rented room, usually in a house | **room** / **a room for rent** |
| Tòa nhà / Tòa chung cư | multi-floor building | **building** / **apartment building** / **complex** |
| Nhà trọ | rooming house with multiple tenants | **rooming house** / **boarding house** |
| Nhà riêng | standalone private house | **house** / **single-family home** |
| Tầng / Lầu | floor/storey of a building | **floor** (ground floor, second floor) |
| Khu dân cư | residential area/complex | **complex** / **community** / **neighborhood** |

- Teaching note: `căn hộ` is the most common rental term for Vietnamese learners living
  abroad — the English equivalent is **"apartment"** (North America) or **"flat"**
  (UK/Australia). When in doubt, **"apartment"** works worldwide.
- **"Unit"** is the building-management word for an individual apartment inside a
  larger building — essential for maintenance requests and mail pickup: "Unit 12,"
  "my unit," "the unit upstairs."
- **"Complex"** names the whole development: "I live in an apartment complex on
  Main Street."

### 2.2 "Phòng" traps — room vs. apartment

`Phòng` in Vietnamese covers both a room inside an apartment AND any rental space. In
English, the type of space matters.

| VN | EN✗ | EN✓ | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tôi thuê một phòng. | "I rent one room." ✗ (if it's an apartment) | **"I rent an apartment."** / **"I rent a studio."** | "room" means a single room inside a larger unit |
| Phòng ngủ | "sleeping room" | **"bedroom"** | fixed compound word, not classifier + noun |
| Phòng tắm | "bathing room" | **"bathroom"** | fixed compound |
| Phòng bếp / Bếp | "kitchen room" | **"kitchen"** | fixed compound; "kitchen room" is not English |
| Phòng khách | "guest room" | **"living room"** | not "guest room" (that means a spare bedroom for visitors) |

- **"I rent a room"** is correct when you genuinely rent one room in a shared house —
  your bedroom, with a shared kitchen and bathroom. It is not the phrase for a whole
  apartment.
- **Studio apartment** (`căn hộ studio`) = one-room unit where sleeping, living, and
  kitchen are combined — important for learners looking at budget rentals.

### 2.3 Counting housing units

VN uses classifiers in counting; English uses the number + noun.

| VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Hai căn hộ | "Two units apartment" | **"Two apartments"** |
| Ba phòng ngủ | "Three bedrooms" | **"Three bedrooms"** ✓ — this one transfers well |
| Một tòa chung cư | "One building apartment" | **"One apartment building"** / **"A building"** |
| Tầng hai | "Floor second" | **"The second floor"** / **"The second floor"** |

- **"A one-bedroom apartment"** (`căn hộ một phòng ngủ`) is a natural English compound:
  "I'm looking for a one-bedroom apartment" — drill this pattern as a single chunk.
- **"A two-bedroom"** without "apartment" is understood in context — learners can
  trust this shortform once they know the full compound.

## 3. Utilities vocabulary — điện nước and beyond

### 3.1 "Điện nước" is not "utilities" alone

`Điện nước` literally bundles electricity (`điện`) and water (`nước`) as a single
concept — all household services in one word. In English, **utilities** is the umbrella
word, but each utility is managed separately.

| VN term | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Điện nước | "electricity-water" (literal) | **"utilities"** (umbrella) or name them: "electricity and water" |
| Đăng ký điện nước | "register electricity-water" | **"set up utilities"** / **"set up electricity and gas"** |
| Tiền điện nước | "electricity-water money" | **"utility bills"** / **"the utilities bill"** |
| Điện nước đã bao gồm | "electricity-water already included" | **"utilities included"** |
| Tiền điện | "electricity money" | **"the electricity bill"** / **"the electric bill"** |
| Tiền ga | "gas money" | **"the gas bill"** |
| Tiền nước | "water money" | **"the water bill"** |

- Key teaching point: in a rental listing, **"utilities included"** is the phrase that
  tells you the landlord pays electricity, gas, and water. "Điện nước included" sounds
  like a product name — use the English phrase in English-language listings.
- Three most important utility words: **electricity**, **gas**, **water**. A learner
  who knows these three can handle any utility setup conversation.

### 3.2 Setting up service — the verb register

`Đăng ký` (register) is the Vietnamese all-purpose word for signing up for any service.
In utility English, several verbs are used:

| Action | VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start new service | Đăng ký lần đầu | "Register electricity." | **"I'd like to set up electricity at my new address."** |
| Start at a new address | Chuyển dịch vụ | "Move the service." | **"I'd like to transfer my service to my new address."** |
| Turn off at old address | Ngưng dịch vụ | "Stop service." | **"I'd like to disconnect service at my old address."** |
| Start at new address | Mở dịch vụ lại | "Open service again." | **"I'd like to reconnect at my new address."** |

- **"Set up"** is the natural verb for starting any account: set up electricity, set up
  internet, set up a phone plan.
- **"Transfer"** keeps the same account but changes the address.
- **"Disconnect"** / **"reconnect"** pair: two dates, two addresses. Giving both in one
  message avoids paying for an empty apartment.

### 3.3 Internet and phone plan vocabulary

`Mạng internet` and `gói cước` have direct English equivalents that learners should know
as chunks.

| VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Lắp mạng internet | "Install net." | **"Get internet installed."** / **"Set up home internet."** |
| Mạng mạnh | "Strong net." | **"Fast internet."** / **"High-speed internet."** |
| Gói cước | "Package fare." | **"A plan"** — "a monthly plan," "a prepaid plan" |
| 4G / dữ liệu | — | **"data"** — "How much data does this plan include?" |
| Kỹ thuật viên | "Technical person." | **"Technician"** / **"installer"** |
| Cửa sổ thời gian | — | **"Appointment window"** — the range of time (e.g., 8 a.m.–noon) |
| Khuyến mãi | "Advertising" (literal confusion) | **"Promotion"** / **"promo"** / **"special offer"** |
| Bộ phát sóng wifi | "Wi-Fi broadcasting machine" | **"Router"** or **"modem"** |

- **"Data"** is the only word that works for mobile internet in English — not "4G,"
  not "megabytes" (in conversation). "How much data?" is the question that gets an
  answer in a phone store.
- **"Plan"** replaces `gói` in every context: monthly plan, prepaid plan, unlimited
  plan, family plan.
- **Appointment window**: companies do not give an exact time for installation — they
  give a range ("8 to noon," "1 to 5 p.m."). Asking "What is the appointment window?"
  tells you how long to stay home.

## 4. Verb system gaps in housing language

### 4.1 Missing copula — the "is/are" drop

Vietnamese does not require a linking verb. Housing descriptions drop it systematically.

| VN pattern | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Bồn rửa bị rò. | "The sink leak." / "Sink leaking." | **"The sink is leaking."** |
| Máy nước nóng không hoạt động. | "The water heater not working." | **"The water heater is not working."** |
| Nhà ở tầng ba. | "Apartment on third floor." | **"The apartment is on the third floor."** |
| Điện đã bao gồm. | "Electricity included." | **"Electricity is included."** |
| Căn hộ rộng lắm. | "Apartment very big." | **"The apartment is very big."** |

- Rule: any housing description that uses an adjective or location needs "is" or "are":
  "the sink **is** leaking," "the apartment **is** on the third floor."
- Repair requests especially need this: a message that says "Sink leak" leaves the
  landlord unsure whether it is a question, a past event, or a current problem.
  **"The sink is leaking"** is unambiguous.

### 4.2 Missing progressive — repairs happening now

The present progressive (`is + -ing`) signals something actively wrong right now —
crucial for urgent maintenance language.

| VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Bồn rửa đang rò nước. | "The sink leak water." | **"The sink is leaking."** |
| Máy lạnh đang phát ra tiếng ồn. | "Air conditioner make noise." | **"The air conditioner is making noise."** |
| Ống nước đang bị rỉ. | "Pipe rust." | **"The pipe is rusting."** / **"The pipe is dripping."** |
| Điện đang bị mất. | "Electricity lost." | **"The power is out."** |
| Nước đang chảy vào nhà. | "Water flow in house." | **"Water is coming in."** / **"There's water coming in."** |

- Teaching shortcut: any urgent repair begins with **"The [item] is [action]-ing"** —
  this pattern covers 90% of maintenance requests.
- **"The power is out"** is a fixed phrase for a blackout — "electricity lost" is not
  natural English.

### 4.3 Past tense — when the problem started

`Nó bắt đầu hôm qua` (It started yesterday) needs a past tense that Vietnamese does not
mark on the verb. Maintenance reports need timing.

| VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Hôm qua bị hỏng. | "Yesterday broken." | **"It stopped working yesterday."** |
| Bắt đầu từ tuần trước. | "Start from last week." | **"It started leaking last week."** |
| Đã rò từ tối hôm qua. | "Leak since last night." | **"It has been leaking since last night."** |
| Vừa mới xảy ra. | "Just happen." | **"It just happened."** |

- **"It stopped working"** is the most useful repair timing phrase — covers heaters,
  air conditioners, appliances, anything that was once working.
- **"It has been [verb]-ing since [time]"** signals the problem has been ongoing — more
  urgent than "it started." "It has been leaking since Monday" prompts faster response
  than "It start Monday."

## 5. Prepositions — the "in/at/on" gap

### 5.1 Location: "in" vs. "at" for housing

Vietnamese `ở` covers both "in" and "at" without distinction. English splits them by
context.

| Context | VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside an apartment | Trong căn hộ | "At my apartment" | **"In my apartment"** / **"In my unit"** |
| At the building address | Tại số 12 đường Lê Lợi | — | **"At 12 Le Loi Street"** |
| At a general location | Ở nhà | "In home" | **"At home"** ✓ (idiomatic fixed phrase) |
| In a shared space | Trong bếp chung | "At the shared kitchen" | **"In the shared kitchen"** |
| On a floor | Ở tầng ba | "In third floor" | **"On the third floor"** |

- Fixed phrases: **"at home"** (idiomatic — cannot be changed to "in home"),
  **"in the apartment"** (inside), **"at the building"** (the address or entrance).
- Floors always use **"on"**: "on the second floor," "on the ground floor." Never "in
  the third floor."
- Maintenance requests: **"in my unit"** or **"in my apartment"** for where the problem
  is; **"at my address"** for where the technician should come.

### 5.2 Prepositions with rental transactions

`Đặt cọc`, `trả tiền`, `thuê nhà` each have specific preposition patterns in English.

| VN pattern | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Trả tiền cọc | "Pay the deposit FOR the apartment" ✓ | **"Pay a deposit on the apartment"** (note: "on") |
| Thuê nhà theo tháng | "Rent by month" | **"Rent month-to-month"** (fixed phrase) |
| Hợp đồng thuê trong một năm | "Contract for rent in one year" | **"A one-year lease"** |
| Chuyển ra ngày 31 | "Move out in 31" | **"Move out on the 31st"** (date → "on") |
| Đặt cọc hai tháng tiền thuê | "Deposit two months rent" | **"A two-month deposit"** / **"Two months' rent as deposit"** |

- Date + preposition: always **"on"** — "move out **on** the 31st," "move in **on**
  the first of the month."
- **"Month-to-month"** is a fixed compound — do not translate as "monthly" (which
  means something different: monthly means each month; month-to-month means no fixed
  lease length, renewing automatically one month at a time).

## 6. Polite requests — the ạ/nhé → modal verb translation

### 6.1 Why Vietnamese learners sound blunt or over-formal

VN polite requests use `ạ` (respect particle) and `nhé` (friendly softener) that have
no English word equivalent. Learners either:
- **Drop politeness entirely** (because ạ has no translation) → sounds abrupt
- **Over-compensate with "please, please"** repeated or "I respectfully request" →
  sounds stiff in an everyday rental context

English softens requests through modal verbs, question form, and hedges.

| Level | Vietnamese pattern | EN✓ pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Urgent repair | Anh cho người đến sửa giúp tôi với! | **"Could you send someone to fix this?"** |
| Polite inquiry | Cho tôi hỏi tiền thuê bao nhiêu ạ? | **"Could you tell me how much the rent is?"** |
| Gentle reminder | Anh nhớ sửa cho em nhé. | **"Just wanted to follow up on the repair request."** |
| Requesting a document | Em xin bản copy hợp đồng ạ. | **"Could I get a copy of the lease?"** |
| Making a suggestion | Mình lập lịch dọn dẹp nhé? | **"Can we set up a cleaning schedule?"** |
| Firm boundary | Tôi muốn thông báo trước một tháng ạ. | **"I'd like to give one month's notice."** |

- **"Could you…?"** is the all-purpose polite request: "Could you fix the sink?",
  "Could you confirm the move-in date?", "Could you send a receipt?"
- **"I'd like to…"** expresses your own intention politely: "I'd like to move out on
  the 30th," "I'd like to set up electricity at my new address."
- **"Just wanted to…"** softens a follow-up without sounding pushy: "Just wanted to
  follow up on my maintenance request."

### 6.2 Dạ / Vâng → English acknowledgments

`Dạ` and `vâng` are respectful acknowledgment particles. In English maintenance and
lease conversations, they map to short, warm confirmations.

| VN | EN✓ equivalent |
|---|---|
| Dạ, được ạ. | **"Of course."** / **"That works for me."** |
| Vâng, tôi hiểu rồi. | **"Got it."** / **"Understood."** |
| Dạ, tôi sẽ ở nhà vào buổi sáng. | **"I'll be home in the morning."** |
| Vâng, tôi xác nhận. | **"Confirmed."** / **"I confirm that."** |
| Cảm ơn anh/chị nhiều ạ. | **"Thank you very much."** / **"Really appreciate it."** |

- **"Got it"** is the most useful short acknowledgment in casual housing English — it
  confirms you understand without being either too formal ("I acknowledge your message")
  or too casual ("Cool").
- **"I'll be home"** is the critical phrase for any installation or maintenance visit —
  it confirms your availability without translating `dạ ở nhà ạ` literally.

### 6.3 The polite follow-up when nothing happens

Vietnamese learners often wait silently when a landlord does not reply. English provides
warm, direct follow-up language that is neither aggressive nor passive.

| Situation | EN✓ follow-up |
|---|---|
| Repair not scheduled after 3 days | **"Just wanted to follow up on my maintenance request from Monday."** |
| Deposit not returned after move-out | **"I wanted to check on my security deposit — it's been [X] weeks since I moved out."** |
| Lease copy not received | **"Could you send me a copy of the signed lease when you get a chance?"** |
| No reply to viewing request | **"I'm still interested in the apartment — is it still available?"** |

- **"Just wanted to follow up on…"** is the single most useful housing follow-up opener.
  It is warm, direct, and normal in English landlord-tenant communication.
- **"When you get a chance"** softens a request without making it urgent — appropriate
  for non-emergency items like lease copies or receipts.

## 7. Repair requests — sửa chữa

### 7.1 The repair vocabulary map

`Sửa chữa` is one Vietnamese word for a range of English concepts. Choosing the right
English word matters for how quickly the problem gets addressed.

| VN term | English equivalent | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Sửa chữa (general) | **repair** (noun/verb) | most formal and complete: "request a repair" |
| Sửa | **fix** (verb) | everyday casual: "could you fix the sink?" |
| Bảo trì | **maintenance** | the service or team: "contact maintenance," "a maintenance request" |
| Bị hỏng | **broken** / **not working** | state of the item: "the heater is broken" |
| Bị rò | **leaking** | for water: "the sink is leaking," "there's a leak" |
| Bị nghẹt | **clogged** | for pipes/drains: "the drain is clogged" |
| Mất điện | **power outage** (building-wide) / **the power is out** | |
| Đèn bị hỏng | **the light is out** / **the bulb is burned out** | |
| Máy lạnh bị hỏng | **the air conditioner is not working** / **the AC is broken** | |
| Ống nước bị rỉ | **the pipe is dripping** / **there's a drip from the pipe** | |

- Vocabulary teaching rule: use **"not working"** for anything mechanical that stopped
  functioning (`heater not working`, `AC not working`) and **"leaking"** for anything
  involving water (`sink leaking`, `pipe leaking`, `roof leaking`). These two cover the
  majority of emergency repairs.
- **"Maintenance request"** is the formal name for a repair report in an apartment
  building — it matters because some buildings have online portals titled "Submit a
  Maintenance Request."

### 7.2 Urgency levels — communicating priority

Not all repairs are equally urgent. English has a natural way to signal priority.

| Urgency | EN✓ language | VN context |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency (same day) | **"This is urgent — there is water leaking onto the floor."** | Bị rò nước nặng, không điện |
| High (within 24 hours) | **"The heat is not working and it's very cold."** | Không có sưởi/lạnh khi cần |
| Routine (within a week) | **"The drain is slow — could someone take a look when convenient?"** | Thoát nước chậm |
| Low (non-urgent) | **"The light bulb in the bathroom has burned out."** | Đèn bị cháy |

- Teaching note: an emergency repair message should have THREE elements: (1) item
  and problem ("the pipe is leaking"), (2) location ("in the bathroom of unit 12"),
  (3) urgency signal ("this is urgent" or "immediately if possible"). Missing any of
  these slows the response.
- **"When convenient"** is the natural softener for non-urgent requests — it signals
  you understand the landlord has multiple tenants and you are not demanding instant
  action for a minor issue.

### 7.3 Offering a photo — a practical move

`Tôi có thể gửi ảnh` (I can send a photo) is a normal and expected part of English
maintenance communication. Learners who know this phrase speed up their repair response.

| VN phrase | EN✓ |
|---|---|
| Tôi có thể chụp ảnh gửi. | **"I can send a photo if that helps."** |
| Đây là ảnh của vấn đề. | **"Here's a photo of the issue."** |
| Tôi có quay video được không? | **"Would a video be helpful?"** |

- A photo message that says only `[image]` reads as incomplete in English maintenance
  contexts. Pair the photo with: **"Here's a photo of [the leak / the clogged drain /
  the broken handle]."**

## 8. Landlord-tenant roles and register

### 8.1 The vocabulary of the rental relationship

Vietnamese kinship terms (`chủ nhà`, `người thuê`) map to specific English nouns.

| VN | EN✓ | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chủ nhà | **landlord** (male or female) / **property manager** (for large buildings) | "My landlord" is the everyday phrase; "property manager" for building management |
| Người thuê | **tenant** (formal) / **renter** (informal) | "I'm the tenant" or "as a tenant, I have the right to…" |
| Ban quản lý | **property management** / **management office** | for large complexes with professional managers |
| Hợp đồng thuê | **lease** / **rental agreement** | "lease" is the standard term; "rental agreement" is more informal |
| Điều khoản | **terms** / **conditions** | "the lease terms," "the conditions of the agreement" |
| Tiền đặt cọc | **security deposit** | fixed English phrase — learners should memorize this chunk |
| Tiền thuê | **rent** | "How much is the rent?" — not "How much is the rent money?" |
| Hàng xóm | **neighbor** | "My neighbor upstairs is loud." |
| Đơn xin thuê | **rental application** | the paperwork to apply for an apartment |

- **"Landlord"** is the word for any owner who rents out property — it is gender-neutral
  in modern English use ("my landlord called," "the landlord's policy").
- **"Tenant"** elevates the conversation toward rights: "As a tenant, I'm entitled to
  a habitable apartment." Knowing this word protects learners in disputes.

### 8.2 The "chủ nhà ơi" problem

`Chủ nhà ơi` is a respectful way to address the landlord in Vietnamese. A literal
translation "Dear house owner" is odd in an English message.

| Vietnamese message opener | EN✓ equivalent |
|---|---|
| Chủ nhà ơi, | **"Hi [name],"** (if you know the name) / **"Hello,"** |
| Kính gửi ban quản lý, | **"Dear Management Team,"** / **"Hello,"** |
| Gửi chủ nhà, | **"Hi,"** / **"Hello,"** (in casual email) |
| Thưa ông/bà [tên], | **"Dear Mr./Ms. [surname],"** |

- In English maintenance messages and emails to landlords, a simple **"Hi,"** is
  appropriate and friendly. "Dear Sir/Madam" is possible but formal.
- **Never use kinship terms** (`sir`, `ma'am` unless in a formal service setting).
  A message to a landlord in English starts with their name if you know it, or "Hi"
  if you don't — not an honorific.

### 8.3 Assertive language in disputes

Vietnamese face-saving norms can make learners too indirect when asserting rights.
English has a register for calm but clear assertion.

| VN (too indirect) | EN (assertive, not aggressive) |
|---|---|
| Có lẽ là anh sẽ sửa sớm? | → **"I'm following up on the repair — it's been two weeks."** |
| Không biết tiền cọc bao giờ trả? | → **"I'm entitled to my deposit back within [X] days under our agreement."** |
| Mình nghĩ có vẻ như tiền điện cao quá... | → **"This charge seems higher than our agreed rate — could you explain it?"** |
| Chắc là... có thể... phòng có vấn đề gì à? | → **"I'd like to schedule a time for you to inspect the issue in my unit."** |

- Teaching rule: in a landlord-tenant dispute, **state the fact, then make the
  request**. Don't embed the fact in softeners that make it sound uncertain. "The
  heater has been broken for a week" is a fact, not an accusation. "Could you send
  someone today?" is a request, not a demand.
- **"I'm entitled to…"** and **"under our agreement…"** are tenant-rights phrases
  that sound professional and factual, not combative.

## 9. Rent and bills vocabulary

### 9.1 The money language of renting

Housing financial terms in English are more specific than in Vietnamese.

| VN term | EN✓ | Key context |
|---|---|---|
| Tiền thuê | **rent** | "The rent is $1,500 a month." Never "rental money." |
| Tiền đặt cọc / Tiền cọc | **security deposit** | always the full phrase; "deposit" alone is understood |
| Tiền cọc thiệt hại | **damage deposit** | same money, different emphasis — protects against damage |
| Tiền tháng đầu + tháng cuối | **first and last month's rent** | common in North American leases |
| Hóa đơn | **bill** | "the electric bill," "the gas bill," "the first bill" |
| Phí dịch vụ | **service fee** / **setup fee** | one-time charge at start |
| Tính theo tỷ lệ / Pro-rated | **prorated** | partial month charge; "Why is my first bill prorated?" |
| Thuế | **taxes** | "taxes and fees" — always plural |
| Giá sau khuyến mãi | **price after the promotion** / **regular price** | ask this when signing up for a bundle |
| Tiền hoàn lại | **refund** | "Is the deposit refundable?" |

- **"Prorated"** is the word learners encounter on their first utility bill and don't
  recognize. It means a partial-period charge — the bill covers only part of the
  billing cycle because you started service mid-month. Teaching this word prevents
  a panicked call to the provider.
- **"First and last month's rent"** is a chunk to memorize — asking "Do you require
  first and last?" shows fluency in rental negotiation.

### 9.2 Reading a lease — high-stakes vocabulary

The lease document contains vocabulary that learners need to recognize, not produce.

| VN gloss | English term | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Hợp đồng có thời hạn | **fixed-term lease** | end date specified; leaving early = penalty |
| Thuê theo tháng | **month-to-month** | no fixed end; either party gives notice |
| Thời gian thông báo trước | **notice period** | how far in advance you must tell the landlord you are leaving |
| Phạt nếu phá vỡ hợp đồng | **early termination fee** | cost of breaking the lease before the end date |
| Phụ lục hợp đồng | **addendum** | extra section added to the standard lease |
| Điều khoản về thú cưng | **pet clause** / **pet addendum** | rules about keeping animals |
| Kiểm tra khi dọn vào | **move-in inspection** | document the apartment's condition before you move in |
| Kiểm tra khi dọn ra | **move-out inspection** | confirm condition to get deposit back |
| Chữ ký | **signature** | "I need your signature on the lease." |

- **"Notice period"** is the legal concept behind `báo trước một tháng` — in English
  it is "I'm giving notice" or "I'm giving one month's notice." The possessive `'s`
  matters: "one **month's** notice," not "one month notice."
- **"Move-in inspection"** is a learner's protection: documenting pre-existing damage
  in writing before moving in prevents being charged for it on departure.

## 11. How to use this in content

- All rows in §10 are candidate `l1InterferenceNotes` for any housing, rental, repair,
  or utilities topic — keep the **friendly-context** voice (name the pattern, warm
  target, never frame it as "wrong").
- **Highest priority for immediate function:** §4.1 missing copula ("The sink is
  leaking") and §6.1 polite request modals ("Could you…?"). These two patterns appear
  in virtually every housing conversation topic. A learner who can say "The sink is
  leaking — could you send someone to fix it?" handles 80% of urgent maintenance
  English.
- **Highest priority for vocabulary lift:** §3.1 utilities vocabulary (điện nước →
  utilities, electricity, gas, water) and §7.1 repair vocabulary (sửa → fix/repair/
  broken/leaking/clogged). These two clusters unlock the entire housing topic category.
- **Biggest confidence gap:** §6.1 polite requests. Learners with solid grammar still
  come across as blunt because they produce "Fix the sink" instead of "Could you send
  someone to fix the sink?" One modal-verb drilling session on housing requests changes
  how they are perceived by landlords and management.
- **Cultural bridge:** §8.3 assertive language. Vietnamese face-saving norms cause
  learners to express clear tenant-rights claims so softly that landlords miss them.
  Teaching the pattern "state the fact, then make the request" — "The heater has been
  broken for a week. Could you send someone today?" — gives learners the tool to be
  heard without feeling rude.
- **Lease literacy note:** §9.2 is not production content but recognition content.
  Learners need to read their lease, not write it. Author these notes as "you will see
  this word in your lease" rather than "say this." The goal is comprehension that
  protects the learner.
- Three-format combo per topic: grammar note (missing copula / progressive) + vocabulary
  note (EN = VN gloss) + bilingual mirror sentence (full tone-marked VN ↔ natural EN).
- Always author VN with full diacritics (`căn hộ`, `tiền thuê`, `điện nước`, `sửa chữa`,
  `đặt cọc`, `chủ nhà`, `kỹ thuật viên`, `bảo trì`, `hóa đơn`, `thoát nước`, `ống
  nước`, `máy lạnh`) — the learner's anchor.
- §6.3 follow-up language is an easy win: learners who already know the request form
  but wait passively. A single drill on "Just wanted to follow up on…" changes how
  quickly they resolve housing issues in English-speaking countries.
