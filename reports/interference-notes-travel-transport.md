# Vietnamese → English Interference Notes — Travel & Transport Cluster

**Author:** A2 · **Scope:** the Travel & Transport speak-topic cluster
(`travel.ts`, `airport.ts`, `transportation.ts` — booking transport, navigating
airports, asking directions, buying tickets, ride-sharing, public transit, describing
journeys) · **Audience:** Vietnamese adults traveling abroad, using airports/transit
systems in English-speaking countries, or hosting English-speaking visitors.

Fourth in the series, after `interference-notes-workplace-employment.md`,
`interference-notes-money-banking-bills.md`, and
`interference-notes-healthcare-medical.md`. Same contrastive (L1→L2) format: **VN** =
Vietnamese source · **EN✗** = typical learner output · **EN✓** = natural English
target. MercyBlade principle: interference as **friendly context, never a scolding**;
the goal is a confident, mobile voice — someone who can board a plane, hail a cab, and
navigate a foreign city without freezing.

This cluster is high-yield for diaspora travelers and study-abroad learners. It stacks:
directional/locative vocabulary, transportation-modal verbs, polite request forms,
number precision (fares, times, platforms), and the "ask + confirm back" discipline
that keeps travelers safe.

---

## 1. Why these errors happen — structural facts specific to this domain

The five base facts from the Workplace writeup (no articles, no inflection, no
auxiliaries, topic-prominent, rank address) all apply here. Three more patterns are
especially loud in travel language:

1. **VN direction and location is absolute, not relative.** VN often describes location
   with cardinal points (`phía bắc`, `phía nam`) or landmark anchoring (`cạnh cái cây`
   = "next to that tree"). English transit uses **relative** directional chains
   (`turn left … then right … past the light`). → learners struggle to sequence
   direction steps or drop prepositions.
2. **VN `đi` (go) is the default motion verb.** English breaks motion across many
   modal patterns (`take a taxi`, `ride the bus`, `catch a flight`, `hop on the subway`,
   `transfer at`, `change to line 3`). → learners over-use "go by."
3. **VN numbers are spoken digit-by-digit.** Gate `B14` is spoken in VN as `B một bốn`
   (B one four); in English it is **"B fourteen."** Flight times, platform numbers, and
   terminal letters need the English compound-number habit. → digit-by-digit sounds
   uncertain to native listeners.

## 2. Getting around: motion verbs and transport modals

### 2.1 The `đi bằng / đi xe` → "go by" trap
`đi bằng` (go by means of) maps to a range of English phrases — none of them bare
"go by."

| VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| đi xe buýt | "go by bus" | **take the bus** |
| đi taxi | "go by taxi" | **take / get a taxi / cab** |
| đi tàu điện ngầm | "go by underground" | **take the subway / metro** |
| đi phà | "go by ferry" | **take the ferry** |
| đi xe lửa | "go by train" | **take / catch the train** |
| đi máy bay | "go by plane" | **fly / take a flight** |
| đi bộ | "go by foot" | **walk** |
| đi xe đạp | "go by bicycle" | **cycle / ride a bike** |

- "Go by" is not always wrong (it exists: "travel by train"), but **`take`** is the
  default natural verb for most transit — drill it first.
- VN: *Mình đi xe buýt đến đó.* → EN✗ "We go by bus to there." → EN✓ **"Let's take the
  bus."**

### 2.2 `catch` vs `take` vs `miss`
These three are a cluster of opposites learners mix up.
- **catch** = board just in time, or be in time for a departure: **"If we leave now, we
  can catch the 3:45."**
- **take** = use that mode: **"Take the red line to Central Station."**
- **miss** = fail to board: **"I missed the last train."**

VN has no exact parallels; `kịp` (be on time) and `bỏ lỡ` (miss) handle the
catch/miss pair but aren't attached to the modal verb structure.

### 2.3 Ride-sharing register
- VN: *Mình đặt xe Grab.* → EN✓ **"I'll book an Uber / request a ride."**
- VN: *Tài xế đang tới.* → EN✓ **"The driver is on their way."** / **"My ride is two
  minutes away."**
- VN: *Em ngồi đằng trước được không?* → EN✓ **"Is it okay if I sit in front?"**

## 3. Asking for and giving directions

### 3.1 The asking form
VN direction questions often open with a bare location + `ở đâu` (where is).
- VN: *Ga tàu ở đâu?* → EN✗ "Train station where?" → EN✓ **"Where is the train
  station?"** / **"Could you tell me how to get to the train station?"**
- VN: *Đây có phải là đường đến sân bay không?* → EN✓ **"Is this the right way to the
  airport?"**

### 3.2 Direction verbs and prepositions (the hard cluster)
VN spatial language relies heavily on compass points and landmark anchoring; English
transit directions use a tight set of **relative verbs + prepositions** that don't
map cleanly:

| VN phrase | EN✗ (too literal) | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| đi thẳng | "go straight ahead forward" | **go straight** / **keep going straight** |
| rẽ trái / rẽ phải | "turn to left" | **turn left** / **turn right** (no "to") |
| qua ngã tư | "pass cross road" | **go through / past the intersection** |
| đối diện | "face to face with" | **across from** / **opposite** |
| cạnh (bên cạnh) | "beside near" | **next to** / **beside** |
| góc đường | "corner of street" | **on the corner of** |
| đầu đường | "head of street" | **at the end of the street** |
| khoảng X phút đi bộ | "about X minute walk" | **about a X-minute walk** (hyphen, singular) |

- Compound directions need sequencing: **"Go straight, turn left at the light, then
  it's on your right."** Each step is a complete imperative.
- Distance time: VN *khoảng năm phút* → EN✗ "around five minute walk" → EN✓ **"about
  a five-minute walk"** (article + hyphenated modifier + singular noun).

### 3.3 Thanking for directions + confirming
- EN✓ **"So it's straight on, then left at the lights — is that right?"** (confirm-back
  is safe; it doesn't signal distrust, it signals care).
- **"Got it — thank you!"** / **"I think I can find it from here."**

## 4. Airport and flight language

### 4.1 Check-in and boarding vocabulary
VN `làm thủ tục` (do procedures) covers the entire check-in flow; English splits it:

| Stage | EN✓ phrase |
|---|---|
| **làm thủ tục check-in** | **check in** (verb); **check-in counter** (noun) |
| **ký gửi hành lý** | **check your luggage / bags** (verb); **checked baggage** (noun) |
| **hành lý xách tay** | **carry-on / hand luggage** |
| **lên máy bay** | **board the plane** |
| **cổng ra máy bay** | **gate** (always gate, never "door" or "exit") |
| **đợi lấy hành lý** | **baggage claim** |
| **kiểm tra hải quan** | **customs** |
| **kiểm tra hộ chiếu** | **passport control / immigration** |

- VN: *Cổng số bao nhiêu?* → EN✗ "Which door?" → EN✓ **"Which gate?"** / **"What gate
  are we at?"**
- VN: *Mình có thể mang thêm hành lý không?* → EN✓ **"Can I add a bag?"** / **"Is there
  an extra baggage fee?"**

### 4.2 Numbers: gates, terminals, flights, times
- Gate `B14` → EN✓ **"Gate B fourteen"** (not "B one four").
- Flight `VN230` → EN✓ **"Flight VN two thirty"** (hundreds pattern).
- Time `14:55` → EN✓ **"Two fifty-five PM"** / **"A quarter to three"** (12-hour clock
  is universal in English airports; 24-hour is display-only).
- VN habit of digit-by-digit (`một bốn năm năm`) reads as hesitant — compound numbers
  are the fluency marker here.

### 4.3 Delays and rebooking register
- VN: *Máy bay trễ bao lâu?* → EN✓ **"How delayed is the flight?"** / **"What's the
  new departure time?"**
- VN: *Em muốn đổi vé.* → EN✓ **"I'd like to change my flight."** / **"Can I rebook
  on the next available flight?"**
- Compensation: **"I missed my connection because of the delay — can you help me?"**
  Lead with the fact, then the request; don't apologize for something the airline
  caused.

### 4.4 Security and customs register
- VN: *Em có mang theo chất lỏng.* → EN✓ **"I have some liquids in my bag."** (declare
  proactively; passive acknowledgment is safer than waiting to be asked).
- VN: *Đây là sữa cho bé.* → EN✓ **"This is formula / breast milk for my baby."**
- Customs declaration: **"I have nothing to declare"** / **"I have some local
  currency — do I need to declare it?"** (direct, calm, no over-explaining).

## 5. Buying tickets and navigating stations

### 5.1 `mua vé` ticket-purchase register
- VN: *Em muốn mua vé.* → EN✓ **"I'd like a ticket to …"** / **"One ticket to …,
  please."**
- VN: *Vé khứ hồi hay một chiều?* → EN✓ **"Return or single?"** (UK) / **"Round trip
  or one way?"** (US). Both forms exist; know both.
- VN: *Bao nhiêu tiền?* → EN✓ **"How much is it?"** / **"What's the fare to …?"**

### 5.2 Platform, line, and transfer
- VN: *Tàu nào tới ga trung tâm?* → EN✓ **"Which train goes to Central Station?"** /
  **"Which line do I take?"**
- VN: *Em phải đổi tàu không?* → EN✓ **"Do I need to transfer?"** / **"Do I need to
  change trains?"** (`change` not "transfer" in most EN-UK usage; "transfer" = US/tech).
- **Platform:** "**Which platform** does the 09:15 to Manchester leave from?" (not "road"
  or "way" — always "platform" in train/subway).

### 5.3 Taxi and ride-share negotiations
- VN: *Anh biết đường Pasteur không?* → EN✓ **"Do you know Pasteur Street?"** /
  **"I'm going to 45 Pasteur Street."** (address first, not "Can you drive to …").
- VN: *Tiền theo đồng hồ hay giá cố định?* → EN✓ **"Is it metered or a fixed fare?"**
- VN: *Có thể nhanh hơn không, em trễ rồi.* → EN✓ **"I'm running late — could we take
  a quicker route if possible?"** (polite hedge; don't command the driver's route).
- Change: **"Keep the change"** = don't need the coins; VN learners sometimes translate
  *không cần thối lại* too literally as "no need change back."

## 6. Prepositions: `on`, `in`, `at` for transport locations

This is the highest-frequency trip-up in this cluster.

| Location | EN✓ preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| plane, bus, train, ferry | **on** | "on the plane", "on the bus" |
| car, taxi, small boat | **in** | "in the car", "in a cab" |
| station, airport, stop | **at** | "at the station", "at the airport" |
| arrival/departure | **at** (time) + **in** (city) | "arrives at 6", "lands in Tokyo" |
| seat / row | **in** | "in seat 24A", "in row 12" |

- VN: *Anh ở trên xe buýt.* → EN✗ "I am in the bus." → EN✓ **"I'm on the bus."**
- VN: *Anh ở dưới tàu.* → EN✗ "I am below the ship." → EN✓ **"I'm on the ship"** /
  **"I'm on board."** (VN `dưới` = deck below / inside; EN ignores deck level).
- **on time** vs **in time**: "The train was **on time**" (as scheduled) vs "We got
  there **in time** to board" (early enough).

## 7. Register: asking, confirming, and recovering gracefully

### 7.1 Asking a stranger
VN can omit the introduction; English travel requests work better with a short softener.
- EN✓ **"Excuse me, could you tell me where the exit is?"**
- EN✓ **"Sorry to bother you — I'm looking for gate C12."**
- The softener signals politeness; dropping it ("Gate C12 where?") sounds abrupt.

### 7.2 Confirming understanding
Like the clinical hand-off, travel confirm-back prevents wrong trains.
- EN✓ **"So I take the blue line to City Hall, then transfer to the green line — is
  that right?"**
- EN✓ **"You said platform 3 — did I hear that correctly?"**

### 7.3 "I'm lost / I don't understand" — recovering without shame
VN face-culture makes admitting confusion hard. English travel culture rewards
direct admission.
- EN✓ **"I'm lost — could you point me toward the taxi stand?"**
- EN✓ **"I'm sorry, I didn't catch that — could you say it again more slowly?"**
- EN✓ **"Could you write it down for me?"** (extremely useful when accent + noise
  compound the problem).

### 7.4 Kinship address → "sir / ma'am / excuse me"
There is no English equivalent to `anh/chị/em` for addressing strangers. The default:
- **"Excuse me"** to get attention (no pronoun needed).
- **"Sir" / "Ma'am"** for formal service staff (airport, hotel) — and even these are
  fading in many English-speaking countries.
- First names on name badges → use them: **"Thanks, David."**

## 8. Quick-reference table (cluster cheat-sheet)

| Phenomenon | VN cause | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motion verb | `đi bằng` | "go by bus" | "**take** the bus" |
| catch vs miss | no VN parallel | "I lost the train" | "I **missed** the train" |
| Direction preposition | landmark-anchored VN | "turn to left" | "turn **left**" |
| Compound time-distance | bare noun | "five minute walk" | "**a five-minute** walk" |
| Gate vs door | `cổng` = gate/door | "Which door?" | "Which **gate**?" |
| Number reading | digit-by-digit VN habit | "B one four" | "Gate B **fourteen**" |
| On/in transport | mixed VN markers | "in the bus" | "**on** the bus" |
| Transfer/change | `đổi tàu` | "I must change line?" | "**Do I need to** change trains?" |
| Platform | `đường ray`/no term | "Which road?" | "Which **platform**?" |
| Metered vs fixed | `đồng hồ` calque | "meter or fixed?" | "**metered** or **fixed fare**?" |
| Ticket type | two-way/one-way | "two-way ticket" | "**return** / **round-trip** ticket" |
| on time vs in time | one VN phrase `đúng giờ` | "the train in time" | "the train was **on time**" |
| Asking strangers | no softener habit | "Airport bus where?" | "Excuse me, **could you** tell me…?" |
| Admitting confusion | face-loss risk | (silent nodding) | "I'm lost — **could you** point me toward…?" |

## 9. How to use this in content

- Rows are candidate `l1InterferenceNotes` for travel/transport/airport topics —
  keep the **friendly-context** voice (name the pattern, give the warm target, never
  "this is wrong").
- **Prioritize §4.2 (number reading) and §6 (on/in/at)** — these are the highest
  frequency and most noticeable to native speakers; fixing them lifts perceived
  fluency immediately.
- **§7.3 ("I'm lost")** is the confidence unlock for this cluster: many VN learners
  freeze at confusion because asking feels shameful. A single practised recovery phrase
  — "I'm sorry, I didn't catch that, could you repeat more slowly?" — breaks the
  freeze.
- Three-format combo per topic: grammar note + vocabulary note (`EN = VN gloss`) +
  bilingual mirror sentence (full tone-marked VN ↔ natural EN).
- Always author VN with full diacritics (`đi thẳng`, `rẽ trái`, `đổi tàu`,
  `ký gửi hành lý`, `hành lý xách tay`) — the learner's anchor.
