# Vietnamese → English Interference Notes — Workplace & Employment Cluster

**Author:** A9 · **Scope:** the Workplace & Employment speak-topic cluster
(`workJob.ts`, `jobInterview.ts`, `workplace.ts`) · **Audience:** Vietnamese adult
learners using English at work, in job interviews, and with managers/coworkers.

This is a contrastive (L1→L2) reference: it documents *why* a Vietnamese speaker
produces a given English error, the natural English target, and worked examples from
this cluster's real scenarios. It is the "why" behind the per-topic
`l1InterferenceNotes` in the three files above. Notation: **VN** = Vietnamese source ·
**EN✗** = typical learner output · **EN✓** = natural English target.

Guiding principle (MercyBlade): interference is named as **friendly context, never a
grammar scolding**. The goal is a confident, warm, adult voice — not perfection.

---

## 1. Why these errors happen — the five structural facts about Vietnamese

Vietnamese is an isolating, analytic language. Five properties drive most of the
interference in this cluster:

1. **No articles.** Vietnamese has no `a/an/the`. → learners omit them.
2. **No noun inflection.** Nouns don't change for plural; number is shown by
   classifiers/quantifiers or context. → learners drop plural `-s`.
3. **No verb inflection.** Verbs never change for tense, person, or number; time is
   carried by separate words (`đã` past, `đang` progressive, `sẽ` future) or context.
   → learners drop `-ed`, `-s`, `-ing`, and the copula.
4. **Topic-prominent, particle-rich.** Questions and politeness are marked with
   sentence-final particles (`không`, `ạ`, `nhé`) rather than word-order changes or
   auxiliaries. → learners skip subject–auxiliary inversion and `do`-support.
5. **Pervasive kinship/rank address.** Age and status are encoded in pronouns
   (`anh/chị/em/cô/chú`). → learners over-formalize and under-use first names.

Everything in §2–§6 is a surface symptom of one of these five.

## 2. Noun phrase: articles, number, possessive

### 2.1 Articles (`a/an/the`)
VN has no articles, so they are the single most-omitted English element.
- VN: *Hôm nay là ngày đầu của em.* → EN✗ "Today is first day." → EN✓ **"Today is my
  first day."**
- VN: *Em là người chăm chỉ.* → EN✗ "I am hard worker." → EN✓ **"I'm a hard worker."**
- VN: *Em có một điểm yếu.* → EN✗ "I have weakness." → EN✓ **"I have a weakness I'm
  working on."**
- Teaching note: `the` points to a shared, specific thing — "the team", "the schedule",
  "the company" all mean *this* one in front of you.

### 2.2 Plural `-s`
VN nouns don't inflect; "five year" and "many skill" feel complete to the ear.
- VN: *năm năm kinh nghiệm* → EN✗ "five year experience" → EN✓ **"five years of
  experience"** (note: plural `-s` **and** `of`).
- VN: *báo trước một tháng* → EN✗ "one month notice" → EN✓ **"one month's notice."**
- VN: *vài câu hỏi* → EN✗ "a couple of question" → EN✓ **"a couple of questions."**

### 2.3 Possessive `'s`
VN marks possession by word order (`notice của một tháng`); there is no `'s`.
- EN✓ **"two weeks' notice"**, **"one month's notice"** — the apostrophe-s is the
  notice *of* that period. Dropping it ("two week notice") sounds clipped.

## 3. Verb system: tense, aspect, copula

### 3.1 Past `-ed` (finished actions and stories)
VN marks past with `đã`/context, so the verb stays bare.
- VN: *Em làm ở nhà máy hai năm.* → EN✗ "I work at a factory for two years." → EN✓
  **"I worked at a factory for two years."**
- VN: *Em nghỉ việc cũ.* → EN✗ "I leave my last job." → EN✓ **"I left my last job."**
- Story consistency: once one verb is past, the rest follow — EN✗ "I stay calm and I
  fix it" → EN✓ **"I stayed calm and I fixed it."**

### 3.2 Progressive `-ing` (happening now / in progress)
- VN: *Em đang cải thiện quản lý thời gian.* → EN✗ "I improve my time management now."
  → EN✓ **"I'm improving my time management."**
- VN: *Em đang bị trễ.* → EN✗ "I late." → EN✓ **"I'm running late."** ("running" =
  falling behind in time — an idiom, not literal running.)

### 3.3 Present perfect `have + past participle` (experience over time)
VN uses a time phrase only; English adds `have`.
- VN: *Em từng xử lý nhiều tình huống khó.* → EN✓ **"I have handled many hard
  situations."**

### 3.4 Copula "to be" dropped
VN adjectives are predicates by themselves (`ca em mấy giờ` = "shift my what time").
- EN✗ "My shift what time?" → EN✓ **"What time is my shift?"**
- EN✗ "Lease how long?" → EN✓ **"How long is the lease?"**

## 4. Questions, negation, modality

### 4.1 Subject–auxiliary inversion
VN keeps subject–verb order and adds a final particle (`không`).
- VN: *Em hỏi anh một câu được không?* → EN✗ "I ask you a question?" → EN✓ **"Can I ask
  you a quick question?"**
- VN: *Anh có rảnh không?* → EN✓ **"Do you have a minute?"**

### 4.2 `do`-support (do/does/did)
There is no `do`-auxiliary in VN; questions/negatives feel complete without it.
- VN: *Em hủy tự động thanh toán bằng cách nào?* → EN✗ "I cancel autopay how?" → EN✓
  **"How do I cancel autopay?"**
- VN: *Có gói hàng cho phòng em không?* → EN✗ "Package arrive?" → EN✓ **"Did a package
  arrive for my unit?"**

### 4.3 Conditional / hypothetical `would`
- VN: *Trả góp mỗi tháng bao nhiêu?* → EN✓ **"How much would the monthly payment be?"**

### 4.4 The `bị` adversative (≈ passive of misfortune)
VN marks bad things happening to you with `bị`.
- VN: *Thẻ của em bị từ chối.* → EN✗ "My card decline." → EN✓ **"My card was declined."**
- VN: *Tôi bị lạc.* → EN✗ "I am got lost." → EN✓ **"I'm lost."** (simple adjective, no
  extra "got").

## 5. Prepositions, phrasal verbs, complementation

### 5.1 Prepositions don't map 1-to-1
- "interested **in** viewing", "good **at** solving", "help **with** a return",
  "transfer **to** my family", "free **on** the weekend", "flight **at** 3."
- VN `lúc 3 giờ` → EN✗ "in 3 o'clock" → EN✓ **"at 3."**

### 5.2 Phrasal verbs (workplace staples)
A single VN verb often becomes a verb+particle in English:
| VN idea | EN✓ phrasal | Cluster use |
|---|---|---|
| đi làm (be at work) | **come in** | "I can't come in today." |
| báo nghỉ ốm | **call in sick** | "I need to call in sick." |
| xin nghỉ thứ Sáu | **take Friday off** | "Can I take next Friday off?" |
| cài đặt | **set up** | "set up autopay" |
| lấy / nhận | **pick up** | "pick up the keys" |
| lấy lại | **get … back** | "get the deposit back" |
| chia tiền | **split** | "split the rent / the bills" |
- Word order trap: the object splits the phrasal — EN✗ "take off Friday" → EN✓ **"take
  Friday off."**

### 5.3 Verb complementation: `to`-infinitive vs gerund
- After `want`, English needs `to`: VN *muốn học thêm* → EN✗ "I want learn more" → EN✓
  **"I want to learn more."**
- After `good at`, English needs `-ing`: **"I'm good at solving problems."**
- "best way" takes a `to`-infinitive: **"the best way to get to the airport"** (not
  "for go").

## 6. Register, face, and pragmatics (the high-impact layer)

Grammar errors rarely block communication; **pragmatic** mismatches do the most social
damage — and the most good when fixed.

### 6.1 Rank/age address → first names
VN encodes age and status in every pronoun (`anh/chị/em`). English workplaces default
to **first names for everyone, including managers**.
- EN✓ "Hi, I'm Linh" to anyone on the team is normal, not rude. There is no English
  word that carries `em`'s deference — warmth comes from tone, not pronoun choice.

### 6.2 Modesty / face → confident, not boastful
VN politeness favors self-deprecation (`tiếng Anh em chưa tốt`).
- In an interview, EN✓ **"I'm still improving my English, and I learn fast"** beats "My
  English is bad." Saying a strength plainly — **"I'm good at solving problems"** — is
  expected, not arrogant.

### 6.3 Softeners and over-apology
VN softens with final particles (`ạ`, `nhé`) that can't be transferred, so learners
either sound blunt or over-stack "please/sorry."
- One softener is enough: **"Could you help me?"**, **"Would you mind …?"**,
  **"I'm sorry I'm running late — I'll be there by 9:15."** A long apology reads as
  anxious; the *new arrival time* is the useful part.

### 6.4 Small-talk boundaries
VN friendliness can include age, marital status, salary, or family questions.
- Safe English workplace small talk: **weekend, lunch, weather, plans.** Rhythm =
  short answer + one return question: "It was quiet. **How about you?**"

### 6.5 Money and bad-news framing
- Pay questions are acceptable in English when framed neutrally: **"I have a question
  about my paycheck."** Lead with the fact ("some hours are missing"), not blame.
- Leaving a job: forward-looking, not complaint — **"I left to look for more room to
  grow."**

## 7. Quick-reference table (cluster cheat-sheet)

| Phenomenon | VN cause | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Article | no articles | "I am hard worker" | "I'm **a** hard worker" |
| Plural | no inflection | "five year experience" | "five **years of** experience" |
| Possessive | word-order only | "two week notice" | "two **weeks'** notice" |
| Past | `đã`/context | "I leave my last job" | "I **left** my last job" |
| Progressive | `đang` | "I late" | "I**'m running** late" |
| Copula | adj-predicate | "My shift what time?" | "What time **is** my shift?" |
| Inversion | final particle | "I ask you?" | "**Can I** ask you?" |
| do-support | no auxiliary | "I cancel how?" | "**How do I** cancel?" |
| `bị` passive | adversative `bị` | "My card decline" | "My card **was** declined" |
| Preposition | no 1-to-1 | "in 3 o'clock" | "**at** 3" |
| Phrasal split | single verb | "take off Friday" | "take Friday **off**" |
| Complement | no `to`/`-ing` rule | "want learn" | "want **to** learn" |
| Address | rank pronouns | over-formal titles | first names |
| Face | modesty norm | "My English is bad" | "I'm still improving, and I learn fast" |

## 8. How to use this in content

- Each row above is a candidate `l1InterferenceNotes` entry for a workplace/interview
  topic; keep the **friendly-context** voice (name the pattern, give the warm target,
  never "this is wrong").
- Pair a grammar note with a **vocabulary** note (EN = VN gloss) and a **bilingual
  mirror** (full VN↔EN sentence) — the three-format combo (shipped in the
  workJob/jobInterview/phoneCall deepen) covers structure, lexis, and a usable model
  line at once.
- Diacritics are load-bearing: always author VN source with full tone marks
  (`chăm chỉ`, `báo trước`, `nghỉ phép`) — they are the learner's anchor.
