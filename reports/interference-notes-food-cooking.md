**Author:** A3 · **Scope:** the Food & Cooking speak-topic cluster
(`foodOrdering.ts`, `restaurantWorkerEnglish.ts` — ordering at cafés, fast-food
counters, food stalls, bakeries, and sit-down restaurants; asking about menus,
customizing orders, allergies, bills, delivery, takeout, and table service) ·
**Audience:** Vietnamese adults ordering food in English environments, and VN workers
in restaurant/café settings using English with guests.

Fifth in the series, after `interference-notes-workplace-employment.md`,
`interference-notes-money-banking-bills.md`, `interference-notes-healthcare-medical.md`,
and `interference-notes-travel-transport.md`. Same contrastive (L1→L2) format: **VN** =
Vietnamese source · **EN✗** = typical learner output · **EN✓** = natural English
target. MercyBlade principle: interference as **friendly context, never a scolding**;
the goal is a confident, warm adult voice — someone who can order confidently, flag an
allergy safely, and handle a wrong dish without freezing.

This cluster is high-frequency for diaspora learners, study-abroad students, and VN
workers in overseas hospitality. It stacks: count/mass noun distinctions, food-specific
measure words, ordering frames (`I'll have / Can I get`), modifier stacking (`less ice /
no onions / on the side`), complaint repair, and the cultural gap around tipping and
direct service communication.

## 1. Why these errors happen — structural facts specific to this domain

The five base facts from the Workplace writeup (no articles, no inflection, no
auxiliaries, topic-prominent, rank address) all apply here. Four more patterns are
especially loud in food and restaurant language:

1. **VN nouns carry no count/mass distinction.** `cà phê` (coffee), `bánh mì` (bread),
   `nước` (water) are the same form whether one cup, two loaves, or a glass. English
   treats many food items as **mass nouns** (uncountable) requiring measure words —
   `a cup of coffee`, `a loaf of bread`, `a glass of water` — while others are **count
   nouns** (`two coffees`, `three rolls`). → learners use bare nouns ("two coffee",
   "a bread") or omit the measure word.

2. **VN ordering is `cho tôi` + item.** The phrase `cho tôi` (give me) is the default
   order frame; transferred directly to English it produces an abrupt bare imperative.
   English ordering softens with modal frames: `I'll have…`, `Can I get…`, `Could I
   have…`. → learners either over-copy `give me` or produce a bare noun without a frame.

3. **VN modifier order is post-nominal.** Vietnamese stacks modifiers after the noun
   (`cà phê đá ít đường` = "coffee ice less sweet"). English modifier chains are
   pre-nominal or use prepositional phrases (`less-sweet iced coffee`, `iced coffee with
   less sugar`). → learners produce garbled modifier stacks or omit connectors.

4. **VN complaint language is highly indirect.** Face culture makes direct complaint
   difficult; a Vietnamese speaker may stay silent or use very indirect framing (`hình
   như món này không phải của em` ≈ "it seems like maybe this dish isn't mine?"). English
   restaurant staff expect a clear, polite statement — `I think there's been a mix-up`.
   → learners either freeze or over-apologize, making the problem harder to fix.

## 2. Count nouns, mass nouns, and measure words

### 2.1 The countable/uncountable split for common food items

| Food item | Grammar type | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| coffee (in a café) | **count** (each serving) | "two coffee" | **"two coffees"** |
| bread (loaf form) | **mass** | "a bread" | **"a loaf of bread"** |
| bread (a slice) | mass + measure | "one bread" | **"a slice of bread"** |
| rice | **mass** | "a rice" | **"some rice"** / **"a bowl of rice"** |
| water | **mass** | "a water" | **"a glass of water"** / **"some water"** |
| cake (a serving) | **mass** | "one cake" | **"a slice of cake"** / **"a piece of cake"** |
| rolls/buns | **count** | "three roll" | **"three rolls"** |
| eggs | **count** | "two egg" | **"two eggs"** |
| noodles | **mass** (usually) | "a noodle" | **"some noodles"** / **"a bowl of noodles"** |
| soup | **mass** | "a soup" | **"a bowl of soup"** / **"some soup"** |

- VN: *Cho tôi một ly nước.* → EN✗ "Give me one water." → EN✓ **"Can I get a glass of
  water, please?"**
- VN: *Tôi muốn thêm cơm.* → EN✗ "I want more rice bowl." → EN✓ **"Could I get some
  more rice?"**

### 2.2 Measure words — the handful that matter most

| Measure word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **a cup of** | hot drinks, small drinks | "a cup of tea", "a cup of soup" |
| **a glass of** | cold drinks, juice, water | "a glass of water", "a glass of juice" |
| **a bowl of** | rice, noodles, soup, cereal | "a bowl of noodles", "a bowl of pho" |
| **a slice / piece of** | cake, bread, pie, pizza | "a slice of cake", "a piece of toast" |
| **a loaf of** | whole bread | "a loaf of sourdough" |
| **a dozen** | eggs, rolls | "a dozen eggs", "half a dozen rolls" |
| **an order of** | restaurant quantity | "an order of fries", "an order of wings" |
| **a serving of** | formal/menu | "a serving of vegetables" |

- Teaching note: when in doubt, **a bowl of** covers most VN staples (phở, bún, cháo,
  cơm), and **an order of** covers any menu item at a restaurant.

### 2.3 Plural `-s` on count nouns

VN nouns don't inflect; learners hear the `-s` but feel it's optional.
- VN: *hai cuốn chả giò* → EN✗ "two spring roll" → EN✓ **"two spring rolls"**
- VN: *vài quả trứng* → EN✗ "a couple egg" → EN✓ **"a couple of eggs"**
- Rule of thumb: whenever a number or quantifier appears before a food item, check if
  it needs `-s`. Numbers 2+ almost always trigger it for count nouns.

## 3. Ordering frames — how to start a food order

### 3.1 The `cho tôi` → ordering frames translation

Vietnamese `cho tôi` (give me) is direct and culturally neutral; in English a bare
imperative sounds curt at a café or restaurant counter.

| Register | EN frame | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Warm & natural | **"I'll have…"** | standard restaurant/café order |
| Polite request | **"Can I get…"** / **"Could I get…"** | most contexts, slightly softer |
| Direct but fine | **"One [item], please."** | fast food, stall, quick counter |
| Formal | **"I'd like…"** | sit-down restaurant, phone order |

- VN: *Cho tôi một ly cà phê đá.* → EN✗ "Give me one iced coffee." → EN✓ **"Can I get
  an iced coffee, please?"** / **"I'll have an iced coffee."**
- VN: *Cho tôi số ba.* → EN✗ "Give me number three." → EN✓ **"I'll have the number
  three."** / **"Can I get the number three meal?"**

### 3.2 The closing frame — "That's everything"

Vietnamese orders trail off or end with a particle. English orders need a clean close.
- EN✓ **"That's all, thanks."** / **"That's it."** / **"I think that's everything."**
- Prompt: the server will ask **"Anything else?"** — this is the cue to close cleanly.

### 3.3 `I'd like` vs `I want` — the register gap

`Tôi muốn` maps directly onto `I want`, which is grammatically fine but registers as
blunt in a service context. `I'd like` is the contraction of `I would like` — same
meaning, warmer tone.
- EN✗ "I want the salmon." → EN✓ **"I'd like the salmon, please."**
- Teaching note: neither is wrong, but `I'd like` is the frame to drill first because
  it covers the widest register range (café to fine dining).

## 4. Modifier stacking — customizing an order

### 4.1 The `ít / không` → "less / no" transfer

| VN modifier | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| ít đường | "less sugar, not too sweet" | **"less sugar, please"** / **"not too sweet"** |
| không đá | "no ice, not have ice" | **"no ice, please"** / **"without ice"** |
| ít đá | "less ice" ✓ | **"less ice"** ✓ (this one transfers cleanly) |
| không hành | "not have onion, no onion" | **"no onions, please"** / **"without onions"** |
| thêm phô mai | "more cheese, extra cheese" | **"extra cheese, please"** |
| ít cay | "less spicy" ✓ | **"mild, please"** / **"not too spicy"** (idiomatic) |

- The most natural frame: **"Can I get it with no onions?"** or **"Could I have it not
  too sweet?"** The prep phrase `with / without` is cleaner than stacking adjectives.
- **"On the side"** (= served separately, not mixed in): VN has no direct phrase; this
  is pure lexical acquisition — "dressing on the side", "sauce on the side."

### 4.2 Modifier order in English
English modifier order: **Quantity → Opinion → Size → Temperature → Specific**
- EN✓ **"a large iced coffee with less sugar"** (not "iced large coffee less sugar")
- EN✓ **"two spicy chicken wings with extra sauce"**
- The safest approach: `[frame] + [item] + [with/without] + [modifier]`.

### 4.3 "Hold the…" — a server phrase learners should recognize
Servers use `hold the [ingredient]` to mean `leave it out`.
- Server: **"Hold the onions?"** = `Không hành?`
- Learner doesn't need to say it, but needs to recognize it as confirmation.

## 5. Asking about the menu

### 5.1 What's in it / What comes with it

| VN question | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Cái này có gì? | "This has what?" | **"What's in this?"** / **"What does this come with?"** |
| Đi kèm gì? | "Come along what?" | **"What comes with it?"** |
| Món nào ngon? | "Which dish delicious?" | **"What do you recommend?"** |
| Phần ăn có to không? | "Portion big or not?" | **"How big is it?"** / **"Is it enough to share?"** |
| Cay không? | "Spicy or not?" | **"Is it spicy?"** / **"How spicy is it?"** |
| Có chay không? | "Have vegetarian?" | **"Do you have a vegetarian option?"** |

### 5.2 Describing cooking methods — a vocabulary gap
VN learners often know the food name but not how it's cooked in English:

| VN method | EN✓ |
|---|---|
| chiên / rán | **fried** |
| nướng | **grilled** / **baked** / **roasted** (check which) |
| hấp | **steamed** |
| luộc | **boiled** |
| kho | **braised** (in a clay pot / in sauce) |
| sống / tái | **raw** / **rare** |
| chín hẳn | **well-done** |
| vừa | **medium** |

- Note: `nướng` covers grilled, baked, and roasted — the server or menu will clarify.
  Asking **"Is it grilled or baked?"** is a natural way to distinguish.

## 6. Allergies and dietary needs

### 6.1 The word `dị ứng` → "allergic to / allergy"

`Dị ứng` covers allergic reactions broadly; English splits into noun and adjective forms.
- EN✓ **"I'm allergic to peanuts."** (adjective + `to`)
- EN✓ **"I have a peanut allergy."** (noun form)
- EN✗ "I allergy peanuts." / "I have allergic." — both miss the grammar pivot.
- Safety emphasis: for a real allergy, say it clearly and early: **"It's a serious
  allergy — could you check with the kitchen?"** Staff would rather hear it plainly.

### 6.2 Diet labels — precision matters

| VN label | EN✓ term | What it excludes |
|---|---|---|
| ăn chay (Phật giáo) | **vegan** | ALL animal products |
| ăn chay (trứng, sữa OK) | **vegetarian** / **lacto-ovo vegetarian** | meat and fish only |
| không ăn thịt heo | **no pork** / **pork-free** | pork specifically |
| không ăn thịt bò | **no beef** | beef specifically |
| kiêng gluten | **gluten-free** | wheat, barley, rye |
| không dùng sữa | **dairy-free** / **no dairy** | milk products |

- In English restaurants, `vegetarian` and `vegan` have precise meanings — using the
  right word avoids a dish arriving with broth or eggs the learner didn't want.
- **"Does this contain…?"** is the safest check: **"Does this contain shellfish?"**

### 6.3 `bị` adversative → passive for reactions
VN uses `bị` for bad things that happen to you; the English equivalent in medical/food
allergy talk is the passive or stative verb.
- VN: *Em bị dị ứng.* → EN✗ "I got allergy." → EN✓ **"I'm allergic"** / **"I have an
  allergy."**
- VN: *Em bị kích ứng khi ăn hải sản.* → EN✓ **"Shellfish makes me break out."** /
  **"I have a reaction to shellfish."**

## 7. Complaints and the wrong order

### 7.1 Why VN learners freeze or over-apologize
VN face culture treats pointing out someone's mistake as confrontational. In English
restaurants, the opposite is true — staff **want** to know, and a clear description
lets them fix it fast.

- Freeze pattern: learner eats the wrong dish silently or just says `okay` when asked
  "Is everything alright?"
- Over-apology: "I'm so sorry, but maybe possibly this is perhaps not my order?" —
  the hedge cascade makes the problem hard to parse.

### 7.2 The repair frame: describe, don't blame

| VN approach | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| im lặng (silent) | — | **"Excuse me, I think there might be a mix-up."** |
| Anh/chị làm sai rồi | "You make wrong." | **"I think this might be the wrong order."** |
| Món này nguội | "Dish cold." | **"Sorry, this came out a bit cold — could you warm it up?"** |
| Thiếu khoai tây chiên | "Missing fries." | **"I think the fries might be missing."** |

- Framework: **softener + describe the fact + request.**
  - Softener: `Sorry,` / `Excuse me,` / `I think…` / `I'm not sure, but…`
  - Describe: `this came out cold` / `the order seems wrong` / `this isn't what I ordered`
  - Request (optional): `could you check?` / `could you warm it up?` / `could I get the right dish?`
- Past tense matters: **"I ordered the chicken"** (not "I order") — the `-ed` signals
  the transaction already happened.

### 7.3 "Is everything alright?" — the check-in question
Servers in sit-down restaurants ask this mid-meal. VN learners often say "Yes, okay"
reflexively even when something is wrong. Two responses:
- If it's fine: **"Everything's great, thank you!"**
- If something's off: **"Actually, I think my order might be slightly wrong — I had the
  salmon, not the chicken."** The word `actually` signals a polite pivot.

## 8. The bill, tipping, and payment

### 8.1 `tính tiền` → bill / check

| Context | EN✓ phrase |
|---|---|
| Asking for the bill | **"Could we have the bill, please?"** (UK) / **"Can we get the check?"** (US) |
| Paying for everyone | **"It's on me."** / **"I'll get this one."** |
| Splitting evenly | **"Can we split it four ways?"** / **"Can we go halves?"** (2 people) |
| Separate checks | **"Could we have separate bills / checks?"** |
| Receipt | **"Could I have a receipt, please?"** |

- VN: *Cho tôi tính tiền.* → EN✗ "Give me bill." → EN✓ **"Could we have the bill,
  please?"** / **"Check, please."** (casual is fine at a café).

### 8.2 Tipping — a cultural concept to name
Tipping is not standard in Vietnam; in many English-speaking countries it is expected
(15–20% in the US for sit-down service). VN learners may not know:
- **The tip is usually added by the customer**, not the restaurant.
- **"Is tip included?"** or **"Is service included?"** is a safe check before paying.
- **"Keep the change"** = `không cần thối lại`.
- If paying by card: many payment terminals will prompt for a tip amount — it is not
  rude to select `No tip` or `0%`, but knowing what the prompt means prevents confusion.

### 8.3 `split the bill` vs `go Dutch`
- **"Split the bill"** = divide however the group decides.
- **"Go Dutch"** = each person pays exactly their own share (slightly formal phrase,
  less common in casual speech, but learners will encounter it).
- VN: *Mỗi người trả tiền phần của mình.* → EN✓ **"Let's each pay our own."** / **"Can
  we pay separately?"**

## 9. Register at the café and counter vs sit-down restaurant

### 9.1 Counter/café (casual)
Short, direct sentences are normal. Long politeness hedges slow the queue.
- EN✓ **"Iced latte, please."** / **"One iced latte."** — totally fine at a counter.
- **"For here or to go?"** → answer: **"To go"** / **"For here"** (not a full sentence needed).
- Name for the order: **"Linh"** — one word is the expected response.

### 9.2 Sit-down restaurant (warmer register)
Full sentences signal engagement; monosyllables can read as unfriendly.
- EN✓ **"I'll have the grilled salmon with the seasonal vegetables, please."**
- **"Could we get a few more minutes?"** (still deciding) — the polite frame fits the context.
- Asking for more: **"Could we get some more bread?"** / **"Another water when you
  have a chance?"** (`when you have a chance` = don't rush, which sit-down service appreciates).

### 9.3 Kinship address → server register
VN uses `chị/anh/em` to address waiting staff by age and perceived rank. English:
- Use the server's name if visible on a badge: **"Thanks, Mark."**
- Otherwise: **"Excuse me"** to get attention; no honorific needed.
- **"Ma'am" / "Sir"** is formally correct but feels stiff in casual dining — `Excuse me`
  is safer across all contexts.

## 10. Food-stall and street-food register

### 10.1 When names are hard — pointing saves you
- EN✓ **"Two of these, please."** (point + quantifier) — no name needed.
- EN✓ **"One of those, and one of these."** — VN `cái này / cái kia` maps directly onto
  `this / these / that / those`.
- Counting VN-style (`cái`) → English drops the classifier: just the number + noun or
  just the number + point.

### 10.2 `bao nhiêu tiền` → "How much?"
- EN✓ **"How much is this?"** / **"How much for one?"** / **"How much for two of these?"**
- Cash: **"Do you take card?"** (worth asking; many stalls are cash-only).
- Change: **"Keep the change."** / **"Do you have change for a twenty?"**

## 12. How to use this in content

- Rows are candidate `l1InterferenceNotes` for any food/ordering/restaurant topic —
  keep the **friendly-context** voice (name the pattern, warm target, never "this is
  wrong").
- **Highest priority for fluency lift:** §3.1 ordering frames (`I'll have / Can I get`)
  and §2.1 count/mass nouns — these are the first things native speakers notice.
- **Highest priority for safety:** §6.1 allergy grammar — "I allergy peanuts" can
  fail to trigger a kitchen check; **"I'm allergic to peanuts"** reliably does.
- **Biggest confidence unlock:** §7.2 complaint repair frame — learners who can say
  "I think there's a mix-up" instead of freezing keep their composure in the most
  stressful ordering moments.
- Three-format combo per topic: grammar note + vocabulary note (EN = VN gloss) +
  bilingual mirror sentence (full tone-marked VN ↔ natural EN).
- Always author VN with full diacritics (`đi kèm`, `ít đường`, `không đá`, `dị ứng`,
  `tính tiền`, `chia tiền`, `bao nhiêu tiền`) — the learner's anchor.
- §9 (register) is particularly useful for learners who have decent vocab but default
  to formal full-sentences at a busy counter — adjusting register is the quick win.
