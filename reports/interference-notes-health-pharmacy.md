## 1. Measure words — the deepest pharmacy gap

Vietnamese uses **loại từ (classifiers/measure words)** before every countable noun: a
pill is *một **viên** thuốc*, a packet is *một **gói*** — the measure word is grammatically
required and conveys the physical form of the medicine. English uses a different system:
form-specific **nouns** that are part of the drug name on the label.

### 1.1 The core VN → EN measure-word map

| VN measure word | What it covers | EN pharmacy term |
|---|---|---|
| **viên** | solid unit — pill, tablet, capsule | **tablet** (scored, chewable, extended-release) / **capsule** (gel-cap, hard-shell) / **pill** (informal) |
| **viên nén** | compressed solid | **tablet** specifically |
| **viên nang** | enclosed in a shell | **capsule** |
| **gói** | packet, sachet | **packet** / **sachet** (powder or granules dissolved in water) |
| **ống** | tube (injectable or topical) | **vial** (injectable) / **tube** (cream/gel/ointment) |
| **chai** | bottle | **bottle** (liquid, syrup) / **vial** (small injectable bottle) |
| **lọ** | small bottle or jar | **jar** (cream) / **bottle** / **vial** |
| **tuýp** | squeezable tube | **tube** (toothpaste-like container for cream/gel) |
| **miếng dán** | adhesive patch | **patch** (transdermal — e.g., nicotine patch, pain patch) |
| **bơm tiêm / mũi tiêm** | syringe | **syringe** / **injection** / **shot** |
| **ống hít / bình xịt** | inhaler | **inhaler** / **puffer** (AU/UK informal) / **spray** (nasal) |
| **nhỏ** (as in "nhỏ mắt") | drop (liquid) | **drop** — "eye drops", "ear drops", "nose drops" |
| **viên đặt** | suppository | **suppository** |
| **miếng ngậm** | lozenge, troche | **lozenge** / **throat lozenge** |

**The key interference:** VN learners say *"two viên"* or *"two medicine"* in English,
omitting the form-specific noun. The pharmacist's question "Did you say two **tablets** or
two **capsules**?" reveals why the distinction matters — they look similar but may have
different bioavailability.

- EN✗ "I take two medicine every day." → EN✓ **"I take two tablets every day."**
- EN✗ "This is viên or gói?" → EN✓ **"Is this a capsule or a packet?"**
- EN✗ "One tube medicine, how use?" → EN✓ **"How do I apply this cream?"** (tube → cream/gel/ointment; `apply` not `take`).

### 1.2 Counting on the label

English prescription labels use **numbers + form noun**:
- "Take **2 tablets** by mouth twice daily."
- "Instill **1 drop** in the affected eye three times daily."
- "Apply a **thin layer** of cream to the affected area."
- "Use **1 puff** (100 mcg) twice daily."

Vietnamese labels in VN often say *"uống 2 viên/ngày/lần"*; the English label separates
count, form, route, and frequency into distinct fields. Reading them left-to-right is the
drill.

## 2. Dosage verbs — one verb does not fit all forms

`uống` (drink/swallow) covers most medicines in Vietnamese. English uses a verb grid
matched to the medicine form.

### 2.1 The VN `uống` → EN split

| Medicine form | EN verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Tablet / capsule / pill | **take** | "Take 1 tablet with water." |
| Liquid, syrup | **take** | "Take 10 mL of syrup." (not "drink") |
| Drops (eye/ear/nose) | **instill** (formal) / **put in** / **use** | "Instill 2 drops in the ear." / "Put 2 drops in your eye." |
| Cream / ointment / gel | **apply** | "Apply a thin layer to the affected area." |
| Patch | **apply** / **put on** | "Apply one patch to the upper arm." |
| Inhaler | **use** / **inhale** | "Use 1 puff as needed." / "Inhale slowly and deeply." |
| Nasal spray | **spray** / **use** | "Spray 2 sprays into each nostril." |
| Suppository | **insert** | "Insert 1 suppository rectally at bedtime." |
| Injection | **inject** / **give** / **administer** | (usually healthcare-worker register) |

EN✗ "I drink the eye drop three time." → EN✓ **"I use the eye drops three times a day."**
EN✗ "How drink the cream?" → EN✓ **"How do I apply this cream?"**
EN✗ "I drink one puff." → EN✓ **"I use one puff."**

### 2.2 Route-of-administration vocabulary

Labels include the **route** as a fixed phrase — learners see these and must recognize them:

| Label phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **by mouth** / **orally** | swallow it (= `uống`) |
| **sublingually** | place under the tongue (= `đặt dưới lưỡi`) |
| **topically** | apply to the skin (= `bôi ngoài da`) |
| **intranasally** | into the nose (= `nhỏ/xịt mũi`) |
| **rectally** | via suppository (= `đặt hậu môn`) |

Learners who see "apply topically" and say *"I drink it topically"* are transferring
`uống` across all routes. The label's route phrase is the reliable anchor.

## 3. Frequency and timing — precision language

A9's notes cover frequency basics (`twice a day`, `since/for`). This section deepens the
pharmacy-specific forms that appear on labels and in pharmacist instructions.

### 3.1 Label frequency patterns

| VN phrasing | EN✗ | EN✓ (label-standard) |
|---|---|---|
| *mỗi ngày 2 lần* | "2 time every day" | **"twice daily"** / **"twice a day"** |
| *mỗi ngày 3 lần* | "3 time a day" | **"three times daily"** / **"three times a day"** |
| *mỗi 8 giờ* | "each 8 hour" | **"every 8 hours"** |
| *khi cần* | "when need" | **"as needed"** (abbrev. PRN on the label) |
| *trước bữa ăn* | "before eat" | **"before meals"** |
| *sau bữa ăn* | "after eat" | **"after meals"** / **"with food"** |
| *lúc đi ngủ* | "before sleep" | **"at bedtime"**  |
| *cách ngày* | "skip one day" | **"every other day"** |
| *mỗi tuần một lần* | "one time every week" | **"once a week"** / **"weekly"** |

### 3.2 Duration phrases on the label

- "Take for **10 days**" — Vietnamese `trong 10 ngày` → EN✗ "take 10 day" → EN✓ "**take
  for** 10 days" (`for` is obligatory; the bare number misses it).
- "Take **until finished**" — means complete the whole course even if you feel better.
  `Uống hết thuốc` maps well; the trap is stopping early (especially antibiotics).
- "Use **for up to** 7 days" — *not* mandatory 7 days; `up to` = maximum. EN✗ "use 7
  day" loses the ceiling nuance.

### 3.3 "With food" precision

*Uống kèm bữa ăn* / *uống sau khi ăn* maps to several distinct English forms:
- **"with food"** = take at the same time as eating (to reduce stomach irritation).
- **"after meals"** = after eating (same practical effect but slightly different).
- **"on an empty stomach"** = `khi bụng đói` — at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after
  eating. This is the opposite of "with food" — mixing them up is clinically significant.

## 4. Reading the prescription label — field by field

A US/AU prescription label has a predictable layout. Learners who can parse each field
independently avoid needing to ask the pharmacist to explain the whole label.

```
Rx # 1234567                ← prescription number (say "Rx number one-two-three...")
Patient: Nguyễn Văn A       ← patient name
Dr. Smith                   ← prescriber
Amoxicillin 500 mg          ← drug name + strength
Qty: 30 capsules            ← quantity + form noun (always the form noun here)
Take 1 capsule by mouth     ← verb + count + form + route
3 times daily with food     ← frequency + timing
for 10 days.                ← duration
Refills: 0                  ← refill count
```

### 4.1 Field-by-field interference

**Qty (quantity):** `Qty: 30 capsules` — learners may read `30` alone and miss `capsules`.
The form noun in Qty tells you what type of unit you received. Always read Qty as
"thirty **capsules**."

**Strength (mg/mcg/mL):** `500 mg` = 500 milligrams. `mcg` = micrograms (1/1000 of a mg —
much smaller). Mixing up mg and mcg is a safety error. If unsure: "Is this five hundred
milligrams or micrograms?" Always confirm.

**Refills: 0 vs Refills: 3:** `Refills: 0` means no refills authorized — you need a new
prescription from the doctor. EN✗ "I have no refill, buy again?" → EN✓ "I'm out of
refills — can you contact my doctor for a renewal?"

### 4.2 Storage instructions on the label

| Label phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Keep refrigerated** | `bảo quản trong tủ lạnh` — do not freeze |
| **Store at room temperature** | `bảo quản ở nhiệt độ phòng` |
| **Keep away from light** | `tránh ánh sáng` — often means store in the original container |
| **Keep out of reach of children** | standard safety warning — not a usage instruction |
| **Discard after [date]** | expiry — `hạn sử dụng`; do not use after this date |

## 5. Register at the pharmacy counter

### 5.1 Counter opening phrases — what to say first

The pharmacy technician needs three pieces of information fast: **name, date of birth,
medication**. Vietnamese learners often open with a long polite greeting that delays the
transaction.

| Situation | EN✗ (over-polite or incomplete) | EN✓ (counter-appropriate) |
|---|---|---|
| Pickup | "Good morning, excuse me, I want to take my medicine." | **"Hi — picking up for Nguyễn."** |
| Giving DOB | "My birth is nineteen ninety-five, March." | **"March 15th, 1995."** |
| Refill request | "I want to buy more medicine same as before." | **"I need a refill on [drug name], please."** |
| Asking wait time | "How long I wait?" | **"How long will it take?"** |
| Asking if ready | "Ready yet?" | **"Is my prescription ready?"** |

The counter register is **efficient, not terse** — "Hi, picking up for [last name]" is
warm AND fast. Vietnamese deference (`xin phép`, extended greeting) is natural at home;
at a busy pharmacy counter it delays everyone and may read as uncertain rather than polite.

### 5.2 Phone register — calling the pharmacy

Vietnamese phone calls often open with a long greeting before stating the reason. English
pharmacy phone calls use a shorter opener and state the reason first:
- EN✓ **"Hi, I'm calling to check if my prescription is ready — it's under Nguyễn, N-G-U-Y-E-N."**

Spelling the surname is standard (Vietnamese surnames are often unfamiliar to Anglophone
technicians). Offer to spell: "Should I spell my last name?"

### 5.3 Politeness formula at the counter

Vietnamese uses `cho tôi` (give me / let me have) → EN✗ "Give me the medicine." is too
blunt. Natural counter English:
- **"Could I get my prescription, please?"**
- **"I'd like to pick up my refill."**
- **"Can you check if it's ready?"**

`Could` and `I'd like` are the register markers; learners who say "I want" are not
impolite in most AU/US contexts, but adding `please` or using `I'd like` is safer.

## 6. Allergy, sensitivity, and intolerance — register distinctions

Vietnamese `dị ứng` covers the full English spectrum from **allergy** (immune reaction) to
**intolerance** (digestive) to **sensitivity** (mild reaction). Getting the right word
matters clinically.

| Reaction type | EN term | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Immune-mediated (rash, swelling, anaphylaxis) | **allergy** ("I'm **allergic** to penicillin") | Always flag this first — it is the most serious |
| Digestive upset (nausea, diarrhea) | **intolerance** / "it upsets my stomach" | Less urgent — dose timing or food co-administration often fixes it |
| Mild, non-immune reaction (headache, drowsiness) | **sensitivity** / "it makes me feel..." | Common; less clinical urgency |
| Unknown — learner is unsure | **"I had a bad reaction to [drug]"** | Safe placeholder when unsure of the category |

**Practical rule:** when in doubt, say **"I'm allergic to [substance]"** — pharmacists
treat this as the high-priority flag and will investigate. It is better to over-flag than
to under-flag.

EN✗ "I have allergy with aspirin." → EN✓ **"I'm allergic to aspirin."**
EN✗ "Penicillin makes me vomit — I am allergy." → EN✓ **"Penicillin upsets my stomach
severely — or it may be an allergy. I had to stop it last time."** (Honest description
when unsure: do not guess the category.)

## 7. OTC vs prescription language — knowing which is which

Vietnamese pharmacies (`nhà thuốc`) sell many drugs OTC that are prescription-only in
AU/US/UK. Learners may not know the category has changed.

### 7.1 Key vocabulary

| Term | VN equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **prescription** (Rx) | thuốc theo đơn bác sĩ | Doctor's written/electronic order is required |
| **over-the-counter** (OTC) | thuốc không cần đơn | Buy freely off the shelf |
| **schedule / controlled substance** | thuốc kiểm soát đặc biệt | Strict limits; ID may be required |
| **generic** | thuốc generic / thuốc gốc | Same active ingredient, lower cost |
| **brand name** | thuốc biệt dược | Manufacturer's trademarked version |
| **formulary** | danh mục thuốc bảo hiểm | Insurance-covered drug list |

### 7.2 Asking about availability

EN✗ "I want to buy Valium, can?" → EN✓ **"Do I need a prescription for this, or is it
available over the counter?"** — asks the pharmacist to classify it without assuming.

EN✗ "This medicine have in store?" → EN✓ **"Is this in stock?"** or **"Do you carry
[drug name]?"**

### 7.3 Generic substitution

The pharmacist may say *"The doctor prescribed [brand] but we have the generic — same
medication, lower co-pay. Would you like the generic?"* The correct response:
- Accept: **"Yes, please use the generic."**
- Decline: **"I'd prefer to stay with the brand-name, please."** (Some patients have a
  specific reason — e.g., a formulation their body tolerates better.)

## 8. Emergency escalation — knowing when NOT to wait

A9 covers emergency signals in the worker register. This section covers the **patient**
side: how to say urgency clearly in English so the pharmacist routes them correctly.

### 8.1 Urgency signals worth drilling

| What you feel | Vietnamese description | EN phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Throat swelling / can't swallow | Cổ họng bị sưng, khó nuốt | **"My throat is swelling — I can't swallow."** → call 000/911 |
| Face or tongue swelling | Mặt, lưỡi bị sưng | **"My face/tongue is swelling."** → call 000/911 |
| Trouble breathing | Khó thở | **"I'm having trouble breathing."** → call 000/911 |
| Hives / full-body rash | Nổi mề đay toàn thân | **"I've broken out in hives all over."** → urgent care or 000/911 |
| Severe dizziness/fainting | Chóng mặt dữ dội, sắp ngất | **"I feel like I might faint."** → sit down, tell the pharmacist immediately |
| Mild nausea/drowsiness | Buồn nôn nhẹ, buồn ngủ | **"I feel a bit nauseous / drowsy."** → report to pharmacist; not usually an emergency |

**The practical drill:** if a pharmacist asks "On a scale of one to ten, how bad is it?"
and you answer with a number, follow with a sentence: **"It's a seven — my throat feels
tight."** A number alone is ambiguous; a description anchors it.

### 8.2 When to call 000 (AU) / 911 (US) — the three-word rule

If you can say **"throat swelling," "trouble breathing,"** or **"chest pain,"** those three
phrases trigger an emergency response immediately. Practice them exactly so they come out
under stress.

## 9. Cheat-sheet — quick-reference table

| Phenomenon | VN cause | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Measure word transfer | `viên/gói/ống` as universal | "Two medicine" / "one viên" | "Two **tablets**" / "one **capsule**" |
| Medicine verb | `uống` for all forms | "Drink the cream" / "drink eye drop" | "**Apply** the cream" / "**use** the eye drops" |
| Route label | None in VN equivalent | "Take topically" (incorrect) | "**Apply** topically" |
| Frequency | bare number | "3 time a day" | "**Three times daily**" / "every 8 hours" |
| Duration | bare number | "Take 10 day" | "Take **for** 10 days" |
| Empty stomach | literal | "Stomach empty, take medicine" | "Take on an **empty stomach**" |
| Refill | no English word match | "Buy again medicine" / "re-order" | "**Refill** my prescription" |
| Allergy | `dị ứng` all-in-one | "I have allergy with penicillin" | "I'm **allergic to** penicillin" |
| Counter opener | extended greeting | Long polite preamble | "Hi — picking up for [name]." |
| Allergy vs intolerance | same VN word | "I allergy to dairy" | "**Dairy doesn't agree with me**" (intolerance) |
| OTC / Rx confusion | VN pharmacy norms | "I want buy Valium, can?" | "Do I need a **prescription** for this?" |
| Qty label | number only | "30 — is that 30 day?" | Read as "thirty **capsules**" (form noun in Qty) |
| mg vs mcg | same-looking label | (no label reading habit) | Confirm: "Is that milligrams or micrograms?" |
