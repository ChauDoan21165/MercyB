## 1. Why these errors happen — structural facts specific to this cluster

The five base structural facts (no articles, no inflection, no auxiliaries, topic-prominent,
rank address) all apply here. Four patterns are especially pronounced at a bank or post office:

1. **VN has no indefinite article; EN requires 'a/an' at first mention.** A learner approaching a
   counter says "I want open bank account" — missing both the infinitive marker `to` and the article
   `a`. Both are invisible in Vietnamese but fully load-bearing in English.
2. **VN marks the past with time words, not verb forms.** At the bank: "My card not work yesterday"
   is grammatical in VN logic, but English needs "My card **didn't** work yesterday." This matters
   because bank staff type notes — imprecise tense signals ambiguity about *when* the problem occurred.
3. **VN question formation reverses subject and question word.** "Tài khoản này phí bao nhiêu?" =
   topic first, question word last. English front-loads the question word AND inserts a dummy
   auxiliary: "How much **is** the fee for this account?"
4. **VN `gửi` is one verb for 'send / post / mail / deposit / transfer.'** The single-verb mental
   map produces "I want gửi this" (untranslated freeze), "send this letter" (correct for casual),
   "transfer my brother money" (word order calque), and "deposit money" (correct financial term)
   interchangeably — learners don't know which word the counter staff expect. This section maps
   each `gửi` sub-sense to its English counterpart.

## 2. Articles and noun phrase structure

### 2.1 "I'd like to open a bank account" — three invisible words

The most common opening at a bank counter exposes three gaps at once.

| Element | VN structure | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infinitive marker | *muốn mở* (want + verb) | "I want open" | **"I'd like to open"** |
| Indefinite article | absent in VN | "a bank account" dropped | **"a bank account"** |
| Politeness modal | absent in VN | "I want open bank account" | **"I'd like to open a bank account"** |

Teaching anchor: the three-part opener *"I'd like to [verb] a [noun]"* covers nearly every counter
request: *"I'd like to open a bank account" · "I'd like to send a parcel" · "I'd like to make a
transfer."* Drill the frame before drilling vocabulary.

### 2.2 Which account? Articles before account types

Both account types need `a` or `the` in English; VN drops them.
- EN✗ "I want chequing account." → EN✓ **"I'd like a chequing account"** or
  **"I'd like to open a chequing account."**
- EN✗ "Do I need savings account?" → EN✓ **"Do I need a savings account?"**

### 2.3 Get a receipt, take a receipt

VN *lấy biên lai* = "take / receive the receipt."
- EN✗ "Can I take receipt?" → EN✓ **"Can I get a receipt?"** / **"Could I have a receipt?"**
- At post office: EN✗ "Give me tracking" → EN✓ **"Could I get a tracking number?"** /
  **"Can I have the tracking slip?"**

## 3. Verb system: tense, do-support, and the hypothetical "would"

### 3.1 Past tense at the bank and post office

VN signals past with time words. English changes the verb.
- EN✗ "My card not work yesterday." → EN✓ **"My card didn't work yesterday."**
- EN✗ "I send the parcel last week." → EN✓ **"I sent the parcel last week."**
- EN✗ "The package not arrive yet." → EN✓ **"The package hasn't arrived yet."** (present
  perfect, because the expected arrival window is still relevant)

Teaching anchor: if yesterday's event is being reported as a problem, past simple usually works.
If the expectation is still open ("should have arrived"), present perfect is more natural.

### 3.2 Do-support in present-tense questions

VN question formation does not use an auxiliary. The English bank encounter relies heavily on
do-support for information questions.
- EN✗ "How much fee this account?" → EN✓ **"How much does this account cost?"** /
  **"What's the monthly fee for this account?"**
- EN✗ "What document I need bring?" → EN✓ **"What documents do I need to bring?"**
- EN✗ "How long take international transfer?" → EN✓ **"How long does an international
  transfer take?"**

### 3.3 "Would" for hypotheticals (loans, exchange rates)

When asking about costs that are not yet confirmed, English uses `would`.
- EN✗ "How much is monthly payment for this loan?" → EN✓ **"How much would the monthly
  payment be?"** (loan not yet taken — hypothetical scenario)
- EN✗ "What exchange rate?" → EN✓ **"What's the exchange rate today?"** (current, use `is`)
  vs. **"What exchange rate would I get if I sent €1,000?"** (hypothetical, use `would`)

### 3.4 Passive voice — "was declined"

At the bank, English reports machine and system outcomes in the passive.
- EN✗ "Machine decline my card." → EN✓ **"My card was declined."**
- EN✗ "They reject my application." → EN✓ **"My application was rejected."**

## 4. Question formation and word order

### 4.1 Topic-prominent vs. subject-first questions

VN: *Tài khoản này phí hàng tháng là bao nhiêu?* (topic: account → predicate: how-much)
EN✓: **"How much is the monthly fee for this account?"** (question word → auxiliary → subject → predicate)

VN: *Bưu kiện này gửi đi mất mấy ngày?* (parcel → send → take → how-many-days)
EN✓: **"How long will this parcel take to arrive?"** (question word → auxiliary → subject → verb → direction)

Practice frame for any service-counter question: **"How [much/long/many] + does/is/will + [noun] + verb?"**

### 4.2 "There is/there are" vs. VN "có"

VN *có* = "to have" or "there is/are." Learners map *có* to "have," producing grammatical English
in some contexts but not the banking register.
- EN✗ "Have a fee for this?" → EN✓ **"Is there a fee for this?"** / **"Is there a charge?"**
- EN✗ "Have any parcel for me?" → EN✓ **"Is there a parcel waiting for me?"** /
  **"I'm here to collect a parcel."**
- EN✗ "Have online banking?" → EN✓ **"Is there online banking with this account?"** /
  **"Do you offer online banking?"**

### 4.3 Embedded questions after "ask" / "know" / "wonder"

After reporting verbs, English uses statement word order, not question word order.
- EN✗ "I want know when will arrive the package." → EN✓ **"I want to know when the package
  will arrive."**
- EN✗ "Can you tell me how much costs?" → EN✓ **"Can you tell me how much it costs?"**
- EN✗ "I'd like to know what is the interest rate." → EN✓ **"I'd like to know what the interest
  rate is."** (note: `is` moves after `rate`)

## 5. Prepositions: directional and contextual

### 5.1 Money direction prepositions

| Action | VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| gửi tiền vào | (money-in) | "deposit to my account" | **deposit into / into my savings** |
| rút tiền ra | (money-out) | "withdraw from account" | **withdraw from** (correct) |
| chuyển tiền cho | (transfer to) | "transfer my brother money" | **transfer money to my brother** |
| gửi tiền cho | (send to) | "send money for my family" | **send money to my family** |
| trả hóa đơn qua | (pay via) | "pay by online" | **pay online** (no preposition) |
| trả tiền mặt | (pay cash) | "pay by cash" | **pay in cash** / **pay cash** |
| trả qua thư | (pay via mail) | "pay in mail" | **pay by mail** |

### 5.2 Post office direction prepositions

| Action | VN | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| gửi bưu kiện đến | (send-parcel-to) | "send this parcel to Vietnam" | ✓ (correct structure) |
| gửi sang Mỹ | (send to America) | "send to USA" | **send to the United States / the US** |
| nhận hàng tại | (receive-at) | "collect at post office" | **collect from / pick up from** |
| nhận thông báo qua | (notified via) | "notify in email" | **notify by email** |

### 5.3 Proof, form, and evidence

Collocations with `of` are fixed at service counters.
- **proof of** address / proof of identity / proof of income (NOT "proof for address")
- **copy of** your ID / copy of the invoice (NOT "a copy from your ID")
- **form of** ID (NOT "form for ID")
- **receipt for** the payment / tracking number for the parcel

## 6. Domain vocabulary clusters

### 6.1 Banking: account opening (mở tài khoản)

| VN concept | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| mở tài khoản | "open account" | **open an account / open a bank account** |
| tài khoản thanh toán | "account for payment" | **chequing account** (AU/CA/UK) / **checking account** (US) |
| tài khoản tiết kiệm | "save account" | **savings account** |
| thẻ ghi nợ | "debit" / "debt card" | **debit card** |
| phí hàng tháng | "fee every month" | **monthly fee** |
| số dư tối thiểu | "minimum money left" | **minimum balance** |
| giấy tờ tùy thân | "personal papers" | **photo ID / government-issued ID** |
| bằng chứng địa chỉ | "proof for address" | **proof of address** |
| ngân hàng trực tuyến | "online bank" | **online banking** |
| kích hoạt thẻ | "open the card" | **activate the card** |

Teaching note: *proof of address* is a fixed English phrase at every English-speaking bank.
Common acceptable documents: utility bill, bank statement, rental agreement — not a Vietnamese ID
with a VN address. The learner may need to ask: *"What counts as proof of address here?"*

### 6.2 Banking: wire transfer and remittance (chuyển tiền)

Vietnamese *chuyển tiền* covers domestic bank transfer, international wire, and informal remittance
apps. English distinguishes these by the mechanism and destination.

| Scenario | VN | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic app transfer | chuyển khoản điện tử | **transfer / bank transfer / e-transfer** |
| Domestic teller transfer | chuyển khoản tại quầy | **wire transfer (domestic) / direct transfer** |
| International wire | chuyển khoản quốc tế | **international wire transfer** |
| Remittance service | kiều hối | **remittance / money transfer service** |
| Recipient | người nhận | **recipient** |
| SWIFT code | mã SWIFT | **SWIFT code / BIC code** |
| Routing number (US) | số định tuyến | **routing number** |
| BSB number (AU) | mã BSB | **BSB number** |

Critical preposition: *"I'd like to transfer money **to** my brother in Vietnam."*
VN word order (*chuyển tiền anh em*) produces EN✗ *"transfer my brother money"* —
the `to` recipient marker must come after the noun object of the transfer.

Confirmation language after transfer:
- EN✗ "Is it arrive?" → EN✓ **"Has it arrived yet?"** / **"When will it reach the recipient?"**
- EN✗ "Give me number confirm." → EN✓ **"Can I have the confirmation/reference number?"**

### 6.3 Banking: ATM and card interactions

| VN phrase | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| thẻ bị từ chối | "card refuse" / "card reject" | **card was declined** |
| không đủ tiền | "not enough money" | **insufficient funds** (formal) / "I don't have enough in my account" (plain) |
| giới hạn rút tiền | "limit money take" | **daily withdrawal limit** / **withdrawal limit** |
| mã PIN | "PIN number" (redundant) | **PIN** (the N already means Number) — but "PIN number" is accepted in informal speech |
| thẻ hết hạn | "card end time" | **expired card** / **the card has expired** |
| thẻ bị khóa | "card locked" | **the card is blocked / locked** |
| kích hoạt lại | "open again" | **reactivate / unlock the card** |

Register note: *"I'd like to report a problem with my card"* is the ATM-helpline opener.
*"My card doesn't work"* is understood but *"My card was declined at [location/machine]"*
gives the bank all information needed for a system trace.

### 6.4 Banking: formal counter register

Vietnamese service culture uses polite particles (*dạ*, *ạ*, *vâng*) rather than modal verbs
to signal formality. English politeness runs through modal phrases.

| Register level | EN equivalent | Used when |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral/assertive | "I want to…" | Informal; counter staff will understand but it sounds blunt |
| Polite standard | "I'd like to…" | Default counter register — correct for all banking tasks |
| Extra formal | "I was wondering if I could…" | Asking for a fee waiver, escalation, sensitive matters |
| Checking understanding | "Could you explain that again, please?" | When terms or steps are unclear |
| Confirming before leaving | "Just to confirm — [repeat key point], is that right?" | After every transaction |

*Dạ em* as a counter opener translates to *"Hi"* + the polite register of the sentence that
follows. There is no direct one-word equivalent; the whole sentence carries the politeness.

### 6.5 Post office: the "gửi" family (sending verbs)

Vietnamese *gửi* covers four distinct English verbs at the post office:

| VN | EN✓ | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| gửi bưu kiện | **send / post a parcel** | Physical object, domestic |
| gửi thư | **send / post / mail a letter** | Envelope/document |
| gửi quốc tế | **send / mail overseas** | International — "mail" common in US; "post" in AU/UK |
| gửi bảo đảm | **send by registered mail** | Important documents, requires tracking |

Universal opener: *"I'd like to send this [parcel/letter/package]"* — works at any English-speaking
post counter regardless of country.

### 6.6 Post office: service options

Learners freeze when asked *"Which service would you like?"* — they know the concept (nhanh/chậm/có theo dõi)
but not the English label.

| VN label | EN✓ | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Thường (standard) | **standard post / regular mail** | Cheapest, slowest, often no tracking |
| Chuyển phát nhanh | **express post / priority mail** | Next-day to 2-day domestic |
| Có theo dõi | **tracked / registered mail** | Comes with a tracking number |
| Thư bảo đảm | **registered mail** | Tracking + compensation for loss |
| EMS quốc tế | **EMS / Express Mail Service** | Fastest international, ~5–7 days to Vietnam |
| Thư hàng không | **airmail / standard airmail** | International standard, 2–4 weeks, often no tracking |

Practical phrases when uncertain:
- *"What's the cheapest option that includes tracking?"*
- *"How long does express take compared to standard?"*
- *"Does this option come with a tracking number?"*

### 6.7 Post office: customs declaration (tờ khai hải quan)

The customs form is the highest-stress moment at the post office. Learners need to name contents,
give a value, and answer restricted-items questions.

| VN phrase | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| tờ khai hải quan | "custom paper" | **customs declaration form** (CN22 or CN23) |
| giá trị khai báo | "value of inside" | **declared value** |
| nội dung / hàng hóa | "what is inside" | **contents** |
| hàng dễ vỡ | "easy break" | **fragile** |
| hàng bị cấm | "not allow item" | **prohibited / restricted item** |
| phí hải quan | "custom fee" | **customs duty / import duty** |

Filling in the form: *"The contents are [2 shirts and 1 pair of shoes], declared value [AUD 60]."*
Writing only *"gift"* or *"personal items"* without specifics is incorrect and may cause problems
at the destination customs.

Question to ask: *"Are there any restricted items I should know about before I send this to Vietnam?"*

### 6.8 Post office: picking up a parcel

| VN phrase | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| phiếu báo hàng đến | "notification paper" | **collection notice / collection card** |
| lấy hàng tại bưu điện | "take package at post office" | **collect / pick up a parcel from the post office** |
| mã theo dõi | "track number" | **tracking number** |
| ủy quyền | "allow other person" | **authorize someone to collect** |
| chứng minh nhân dân | "identity card" | **photo ID / government-issued ID** |

Lost collection notice: *"I don't have the collection notice, but I have the tracking number — can
you look it up?"* This gives the clerk everything needed without the physical slip.

Authorization: *"I'd like to authorize [name] to collect my parcel on my behalf — do you need a
written note?"* (Answer: yes, signed statement + recipient's ID.)

### 6.9 Post office: registered and certified mail

| VN | EN✓ | Clarification |
|---|---|---|
| thư bảo đảm | **registered mail** | Tracked + loss compensation — highest service level |
| thư có xác nhận | **certified mail** (US) / **registered post** (AU/UK) | Delivery confirmation, may or may not include tracking by country |
| biên lai gửi thư | **lodgement receipt / proof of posting** | Ask for this immediately after handing over the item |
| chữ ký khi nhận | **signature on delivery / signature required** | Recipient must sign; sender gets confirmation |
| khi giao phải ký | "when deliver must sign" | → **"I need them to sign for it"** / **"Can I add signature-on-delivery?"** |

Practical sentence: *"I need to send these documents by registered mail — I need proof of posting
and signature-on-delivery."* This one sentence communicates all three service requirements.

### 6.10 Post office: missing or damaged parcel

| VN phrase | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|
| kiện hàng thất lạc | "parcel lose" | **missing parcel / lost package** |
| khiếu nại / yêu cầu điều tra | "complain" | **lodge an inquiry / file a claim** |
| số tham chiếu | "number reference" | **reference number / inquiry number** |
| bồi thường | "pay back for lose" | **compensation / a refund** |
| đóng gói ban đầu | "first box" | **original packaging** |

Opener: *"I'd like to lodge an inquiry about a package — it was tracked but hasn't arrived."*
The word *inquiry* signals a formal case; *"I want to know where my package is"* is understood
but may not trigger the formal process.

Evidence list when parcel arrived damaged: *"I have photos of the damaged packaging on my phone.
Should I show you now, or email them?"*

## 7. Register, formality, and pragmatics

### 7.1 The "counter register" ladder

Service counters in English-speaking countries have an expected formality level. Vietnamese learners
sometimes swing between too blunt (no modal) and too formal (over-elaborate). The target is
**warm professional**: clear, polite, and efficient.

| Situation | Too blunt | Too formal | EN✓ target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening account | "I want account." | "I was hoping that perhaps I might inquire about the possibility of account opening." | **"Hi, I'd like to open an account."** |
| Asking about fees | "How much fee?" | "Would it be too much trouble to enquire about the monthly charges?" | **"What's the monthly fee for this account?"** |
| Sending parcel | "Send this." | "I would like to formally request the despatch of this parcel." | **"I'd like to send this parcel."** |
| Card problem | "Card no work." | "I have encountered a significant technical malfunction with my payment instrument." | **"My card was declined — can you help me figure out why?"** |
| Asking for repeat | "Again?" / (silence) | "I do beg your pardon, but would you be so kind as to repeat…" | **"Sorry, could you say that again?"** |

### 7.2 "Sorry" and "excuse me" are not interchangeable

VN *xin lỗi* = both *"I'm sorry"* (apology) and *"excuse me"* (interruption or request for attention).
- Counter opener: **"Excuse me"** (getting attention), NOT "Sorry" (which sounds like you've done something wrong).
- Mishearing: **"Sorry?"** with rising intonation = natural one-word request for a repeat in Australian/British English; *"I'm sorry"* with falling intonation = apology for an error. Both work, but the intonation distinguishes them.
- Asking someone to repeat: **"Sorry, could you repeat that?"** or **"I didn't catch that — could you say it again?"**

### 7.3 Privacy language at the bank

Banks require privacy framing. Vietnamese learners sometimes state account or personal numbers freely or, conversely, freeze when asked.
- Counter staff will ask: *"Can you confirm your full name and date of birth?"* — **not** *"What's your password?"*
- If uncertain what information is being requested: *"Is it safe to say that here, or should we go somewhere private?"*
- Never say PIN aloud: *"I won't share my PIN — is there another way to verify?"*

### 7.4 Confirmation loops before leaving

Both bank and post office counters benefit from a final confirm-and-repeat before the learner walks away. This is not rude — it is expected professional behaviour.

- Bank: *"Just to confirm — I've transferred [amount] to [recipient], and the reference number is [X]. Is that right?"*
- Post office: *"So this will arrive in about [5 days], I have the tracking number [X], and you've marked it as fragile. Is there anything else I need to do?"*

Teaching note: this pattern — *"Just to confirm — [repeat key information] — is that right?"* — is a professional communication skill that serves learners in banking, healthcare, legal, and workplace contexts alike.

## 9. How to use these notes in content authoring

**For speak-topic l1InterferenceNotes:** the three highest-leverage points per topic are:
1. The article or preposition that VN omits (account/parcel = indefinite article; transfer/deposit = directional preposition)
2. The question structure shift (do-support, embedded-question word order)
3. The register phrase (counter opener, confirmation loop, politeness modal)

**For conversation directions:** build in a natural confirm step at the end of each banking and post office scenario — it normalizes the double-check habit and prevents learners from walking away with missing information.

**For l1InterferenceNotes in postOffice.ts topics:** the gap is vocabulary (service option names, customs form terms) more than grammar. Teach the *labels* (express, registered, lodgement receipt, declared value) before the grammar — a learner who knows the right word can work around syntax errors; one who doesn't know the word is stuck.

**Honesty principle (MercyBlade):** these notes do not promise learners they will never make mistakes. They identify the most frequent slip points so learners know *where* to focus attention. No fabricated success stories.
