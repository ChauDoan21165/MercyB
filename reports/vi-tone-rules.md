# Vietnamese Tone Rules

These rules govern learner-facing Vietnamese copy for MercyBlade.

## Voice

- Use full Vietnamese diacritics everywhere.
- Write Vietnamese first when the surface is bilingual: `Nhãn tiếng Việt · English label`.
- Keep the tone warm, direct, and adult. Avoid baby-talk, hype, fake praise, and guilt.
- Prefer `bạn` for general learner copy. Use family pronouns only when the relationship is known.
- Use `người Việt`, `người học Việt`, or `người học tiếng Anh tại Việt Nam / trong cộng đồng Việt` when the audience includes both in-country and diaspora learners.

## Labels

- For exam sections, use `Kỹ năng Nghe/Nói/Đọc/Viết · Listening/Speaking/Reading/Writing`.
- For tasks, use `Đề bài`, `Câu hỏi`, `Bài mẫu`, `Từ vựng quan trọng`.
- Avoid English connector words inside Vietnamese labels (`vs`, `prep`, `practice`) unless they are part of a test name or route.

## Learning Feedback

- Name the skill, not the learner's identity: `âm này dễ bị rơi mất`, not `bạn phát âm sai`.
- Use `dễ nhầm`, `dễ bỏ`, `gần rồi`, and `thử lại một lượt` for correction.
- Avoid threat frames in streak and reminder copy: no "sắp mất", "cứu chuỗi", "đừng bỏ lỡ", or guilt language.
- For streaks, frame continuity as rhythm: `nhịp học`, `học liên tiếp`, `ghi nhận hôm nay`.

## Exam Prep

- Be specific about the exam: IELTS band, TOEFL iBT score, TOEIC score, VSTEP B1/B2.
- Do not promise score gains or guaranteed outcomes.
- Use `ước lượng` for estimators, not `dự đoán chắc chắn`.
- For VSTEP, describe B1 as `Bậc 3` and B2 as `Bậc 4`; avoid calling B1 `sơ cấp`.

## Family And Referral Copy

- Make invitations respectful and low-pressure.
- Use `người thân hoặc bạn bè` when the surface can include family and non-family recipients.
- Avoid leaderboard pressure such as `đặt mình lên đầu`; use participation framing instead.
- For family templates, preserve relationship pronouns and avoid assuming gender when the input does not provide it.

## Clinical And Crisis Copy

- In mental-health, crisis, health, pharmacy, or safety copy, make only register-level edits unless there is a factual safety issue.
- Do not soften, intensify, or reinterpret clinical meaning.
- Keep urgency, escalation instructions, medication wording, and safety boundaries semantically unchanged.
