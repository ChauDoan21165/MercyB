# Vietnamese → English Interference Notes — Money, Banking & Bills Cluster

**Author:** A9 · **Scope:** the Money / Banking / Bills speak-topic cluster
(`banking.ts`, `housingRent.ts` deposit/rent topics, bill-paying & transaction
scenarios) · **Audience:** Vietnamese adult learners opening accounts, paying bills,
sending money, asking about loans, and handling a declined card or a wrong charge.

Companion to `interference-notes-workplace-employment.md`. Same contrastive (L1→L2)
format: **VN** = Vietnamese source · **EN✗** = typical learner output · **EN✓** =
natural English target. Same MercyBlade principle — interference is **friendly
context, never a scolding**; the goal is a calm, capable money voice.

This cluster is high-yield because money talk concentrates several VN↔EN gaps at once:
number systems, currency, formal/financial register, hypotheticals (loans), and a
privacy/security layer that has no grammatical equivalent in VN.

---

## 1. Numbers, currency, and amounts (the densest gap)

### 1.1 Reading large numbers
Vietnamese groups by **thousands** with native words (`nghìn/ngàn`, `triệu` = million,
`tỷ` = billion) and does **not** chunk in the English "hundred-thousand / million"
intonation. Learners stall on amounts.
- `1.500` (VN dot = thousands separator) is **1,500** in English (comma). The
  separator flips: VN `1.500.000` = EN `1,500,000`. Mis-reading the separator is a
  frequent, high-stakes error at a bank counter.
- Teaching anchor: read in English groups — "fifteen **hundred**", "one **thousand**
  five hundred", "two **million**." Practice the chunking aloud.

### 1.2 Plural currency
VN nouns don't inflect; `100 đô` feels complete.
- VN: *một trăm đô* → EN✗ "one hundred dollar" → EN✓ **"one hundred dollars."**
- VN: *năm trăm đô* → EN✓ **"five hundred dollars."** Any amount above one takes the
  plural `-s` on the currency.
- "Cents" likewise pluralize; "a dollar **fifty**" = $1.50 (the word "cents" is often
  dropped in speech but the number is plural in writing).

### 1.3 "How much" carries the money meaning
VN: *bao nhiêu tiền* literally = "how much money."
- EN✗ "How much money the rent?" → EN✓ **"How much is the rent?"** — `how much`
  already means money here, so "money" is dropped and the verb `is` is added.
- Word order: VN *tiền nhà bao nhiêu* (rent how-much) → EN✓ **"How much is the rent?"**
  (front the question word + verb).

## 2. Financial vocabulary & collocations (false-friend traps)

VN often uses one general verb/noun where English has a fixed financial term.
| VN idea | EN✗ (calque) | EN✓ (term) |
|---|---|---|
| tiền nhà | "house money" | **rent / monthly rent** |
| tiền cọc | "money keep" | **security deposit / damage deposit** |
| gửi tiền (vào) | "send money in" | **deposit (into)** |
| rút tiền (ra) | "take money" | **withdraw (from)** |
| chuyển khoản | "move account" | **transfer / wire transfer** |
| hóa đơn | "paper of money" | **bill / invoice** |
| phiếu lương | "salary paper" | **paycheck** |
| lãi suất | "interest number" | **interest rate** |
| phí trễ hạn | "late money" | **late fee** |
| miễn phí (bỏ phí) | "delete fee" | **waive the fee** |
| đủ điều kiện | "enough condition" | **eligible** |
| số dư | "remain money" | **balance** |

- `utilities` (điện nước) is **plural and uncountable as a group** — "Are utilities
  included?" not "Is utility included?"
- `parking` (as a service/space) is **uncountable** — "Is there parking?" not "a
  parking." One space = "a parking **spot**."

---

## 3. Directionality: deposit/withdraw and the prepositions

VN `gửi`/`rút` are clear, but English pins direction with **prepositions** that don't
map 1-to-1.
- **deposit INTO** my savings · **withdraw FROM** my chequing — the particle shows
  which way the money moves.
- **transfer/​send money TO** a person: VN word order (*chuyển em trai tiền*) can
  produce EN✗ "transfer my brother money"; EN✓ **"transfer money to my brother."**
- **pay FOR** something vs **pay** someone: "pay **for** the bill" / "pay the
  cashier."

---

## 4. Verb system in money talk

### 4.1 Past tense on completed payments
VN time word carries the past; the verb stays bare.
- VN: *Hôm qua em trả hóa đơn rồi.* → EN✗ "Yesterday I pay the bill." → EN✓
  **"Yesterday I paid the bill."**

### 4.2 The `bị` adversative for failures
- VN: *Thẻ của em bị từ chối.* → EN✗ "My card decline." → EN✓ **"My card was
  declined."**
- VN: *Tài khoản bị trừ tiền.* → EN✓ **"My account was charged."**

### 4.3 `do`-support and existential "there is"
- VN: *Có gói/khoản phí nào không?* → EN✗ "Have a fee?" → EN✓ **"Is there a fee?"**
- VN: *Em hủy tự động thanh toán bằng cách nào?* → EN✓ **"How do I cancel autopay?"**

### 4.4 Hypothetical `would` for loans / quotes
Loan and quote talk is inherently hypothetical; English marks it with `would`.
- VN: *Trả góp mỗi tháng bao nhiêu?* → EN✓ **"How much would the monthly payment be?"**
- VN: *Nếu em trễ thì sao?* → EN✓ **"What happens if I miss a payment?"** (present in
  the `if`-clause, present/future in the result.)

## 5. Money phrasal verbs (high-frequency, hard to guess)

A single VN verb often becomes verb+particle:
| VN idea | EN✓ phrasal | Use |
|---|---|---|
| cài đặt thanh toán tự động | **set up** autopay | "I'd like to set up autopay." |
| lấy lại tiền cọc | **get … back** | "When do I get the deposit back?" |
| trả hết nợ | **pay off** | "pay off the loan early" |
| trích tiền | **take out** | "It takes the payment out on the 1st." |
| chia tiền | **split** | "split the bill / the rent" |
| kiểm tra lại | **double-check** | "Let me double-check the amount." |
- Split-object trap: the amount/object can split the phrasal — "get **the deposit**
  back", "pay **the loan** off."

## 6. Register: privacy, security, and face

This is the layer with **no VN grammatical equivalent** — and the highest real-world
stakes.

### 6.1 Money questions are allowed when framed neutrally
VN politeness can treat direct money questions as slightly rude, so learners go quiet.
- It is normal and expected to ask: **"Could you tell me the salary range?"**,
  **"Is there a monthly fee?"**, **"How much is the deposit, and when do I get it
  back?"** A calm, specific question reads as competent, not pushy.

### 6.2 Security boundaries — never read aloud
English bank/​support calls have a hard norm: **never say your PIN or full password.**
- Useful protective lines: **"I'd rather not share that over the phone."** /
  **"Can you verify me another way?"** Learners should practice *declining* to share,
  not just complying.

### 6.3 Disputes without blame
VN may lead with the person/blame; English leads with the **fact**.
- EN✓ **"There's a charge I don't recognize."** / **"I think some hours are missing
  from my pay."** Then ask for the next step. Neutral framing gets faster help.

### 6.4 "Get it in writing"
A cross-cutting safety habit worth teaching as language:
- **"Could you send that to me in writing?"** / **"Can I get a receipt / a
  confirmation number?"** — protects the learner from mis-remembering amounts and
  dates.

## 7. Quick-reference table (cluster cheat-sheet)

| Phenomenon | VN cause | EN✗ | EN✓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separator | dot = thousands | reads 1.500 as 1.5 | 1,500 = "fifteen hundred" |
| Plural currency | no inflection | "one hundred dollar" | "one hundred **dollars**" |
| How much | *bao nhiêu tiền* | "how much money the rent" | "**How much is** the rent?" |
| Term (false friend) | one general word | "house money" | "**rent**" |
| Direction | `gửi/rút` | "send money in" | "deposit **into**" |
| Recipient order | *chuyển em tiền* | "transfer my brother money" | "transfer money **to** my brother" |
| Past | time-word only | "Yesterday I pay" | "Yesterday I **paid**" |
| `bị` passive | adversative `bị` | "My card decline" | "My card **was declined**" |
| Existential | *có … không* | "Have a fee?" | "**Is there** a fee?" |
| Hypothetical | no `would` | "monthly payment how much" | "How much **would** it be?" |
| Phrasal | single verb | "delete the fee" | "**waive** the fee" / "**get** it **back**" |
| Privacy | no equivalent | reads PIN aloud | "I'd rather not share that" |
| Dispute | blame-first | "You take my money" | "There's a charge I don't recognize" |

  `miễn phí`) — they are the learner's anchor.
